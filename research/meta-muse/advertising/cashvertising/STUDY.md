# Study: "How to Sell SO MUCH That It Feels ILLEGAL" (Little Bit Better)

Video: https://youtu.be/yqyLpX1EFqI (52:17). It is an animated summary of Drew Eric Whitman's book *Cashvertising*, in 35 lessons. Studied 2026-10-24 with Gemini, in seven segments; per-lesson notes with every example are in `forensics/`.

The video gates the 22 headline starters behind a link in its description, so they aren't on screen and aren't reproduced here.

## The 35 lessons, condensed into 9 rules

1. **Sell a core desire (the Life-Force 8).** People act on eight hard-wired wants:
   - survival and longer life;
   - food and drink;
   - freedom from fear, pain and danger;
   - sexual companionship;
   - comfortable living;
   - being superior or winning;
   - caring for and protecting loved ones;
   - social approval.

   Find the one your product serves and build the ad on it. When something doesn't sell, **re-title it, don't remake it**. The publisher Haldeman-Julius did this with his books, and the video's creator took a video from 257 views to 5M views by changing only its title and thumbnail.
2. **Put the biggest benefit in the headline, clearly.** 60% of people read only the headline, and a glance takes in 5–6 words. Be clear, not clever.
   - Clever: "$250 keeps you from getting caught in a sticky web".
   - Clear: "Powerful web pages designed in 24 hours … for $199".
3. **Be extremely specific, and use vivid concrete words.**
   - Specific: "343 kinds of fasteners, 28 types of nails…" beats "hammers, screwdrivers…".
   - Concrete: "Earn $2,750 cash weekly" beats "make lots of money".
   - Make a mental movie that engages several senses.
4. **Prove the value with evidence you didn't make yourself:** reviews, data, endorsements, before-and-after results. The example: selling a clear bag with a $20 bill inside for $1 needs no persuasion. Borrow credibility from people or brands the buyer respects, and show recognisable clients.
5. **Sell the end result and the benefits, not the features.** Ask "what's in it for me?" until you reach the real gain. A vivid example beats a statistic; use both for technical buyers.
6. **Fear, scarcity and urgency work, in measured doses.** The product must clearly solve the fear, and too much fear freezes people. Give real deadlines and limits: "Seating is limited to 50", "Offer expires May 15th".
7. **Remove the risk and the friction.**
   - Offer a long guarantee: a year or a lifetime brings fewer refunds than a 30-day one.
   - Spell out the steps to order.
   - Accept many payment methods and offer payment plans.
   - Ask for the sale.
8. **Test offers, and repeat the ad in variations.** "Free evaluation", "50% off" and "2 for 1" are worth the same on paper but perform differently. People barely notice an ad before it has run about 7 times; vary the face or angle so it doesn't get annoying.
9. **Design for reading.**
   - Show a face looking at the viewer.
   - Make visuals at least half the ad; a photo of the product in use adds attention.
   - Use mixed case, not ALL CAPS (11.8% faster to read).
   - Use dark text on a light background.
   - Leave about 20% empty space.
   - Pick fonts that match the product.
   - Ask questions to open curiosity loops.
   - Pricing: an odd price (.99/.97) signals a deal, a round price signals quality (in a Rutgers study, a $50 dress was rated higher quality than the same dress at $49.99).
   - Long copy outsells short copy for considered buys.

**Caveat:** several statistics are quoted without sources (the 60% figure, "7 times", +13% for photos). Read them as rules of thumb from print-era advertising, not as current platform data.

## Applied to our work

### Chase Scene 101 and other video channels
- **Why chase clips work.** A chase hits two of the eight desires at once: survival and freedom from danger. The zero-view explainers opened on a fact ("Why the fastest animal slows down"), which is curiosity with no survival stakes. In hybrids, **open on the stakes** (the chase), then give the fact.
- **The title and top banner are the headline.** Use the biggest benefit or stake, under 6 words, clear not clever, mixed case.
- **Re-title before remaking.** Long-form videos and Shorts that stall get a new title and thumbnail angle before we make a new one.
- **Thumbnails:** a face (an animal's eyes count) looking at the viewer, about 20% clean margin, one specific number.

### Lane 1, selling video ads to businesses (`../../MONEY-ENGINE.md`)
These lessons are the brief for every ad we make for clients, and for our own offer:
- **Lead with the client's buyer's core desire.**
- **One clear headline with the biggest benefit**, and specifics in the body.
- **Proof** from reviews, numbers or before-and-after.
- **Ten or more hook variants per asset bank**, which covers both "test offers" and "repeat with variation". That variant volume is already our selling point.
- **A real deadline**, and a single clear call to action.

### Etsy listings (`../../side-hustles/etsy-ai-clipart/`)
- Each title leads with the exact keyword and the benefit: "40 Watercolor Mahjong Clipart PNG, Commercial Use".
- The first listing photo shows the designs **in use**, on a card or a tag.
- Round prices on bundles to signal quality.
- Bullet-point the specifics: count, size, licence.

## A sample: an ad for our own video-ad offer

Applying the rules, 20 s, 9:16:

```
[0–2 s] HEADLINE ON SCREEN (benefit, ≤6 words): "10 Video Ads for Your Shop. 72 Hours."
[2–6 s] Mental movie: a scroll-stopping product ad plays (sample from our reel), counter ticks "Ad 1 … Ad 10".
[6–11 s] Specifics + proof: "10 hooks • 3 lengths • captions • music licensed • your logo" — then a real client result or, until we have one, the sample's watch-time stat.
[11–15 s] End result: "Test 10 angles, keep the winner — stop guessing what sells."
[15–18 s] Risk reversal + scarcity: "Love one or you don't pay. 5 launch slots this month."
[18–20 s] Easy buy: "DM 'ADS' — reply in 1 hour."
```

Every claim in this sample has to be true before it runs: the slot count, the guarantee and any results. Leave out anything we can't back.
