### **Lesson 1: Directing Mental Movies**

* **Rule:** Turn simple images into vivid, highly detailed mental pictures by engaging as many of the five senses (taste, smell, touch, hearing, and sight) as possible to boost the intensity and effectiveness of your ad copy.
* **Examples & Visuals:**
  * **Mental Movie #1:** *"Go somewhere and do something"* (Blank movie screen, no imagery).
  * **Mental Movie #2:** *"Go to the kitchen, open the oven, and get food"* (Mental picture of opening an oven).
  * **Mental Movie #3:** *"Go to the kitchen… open the oven and pull out the freshest, crispiest, most delicious hot pizza you’ve ever eaten. Go on, cut yourself a big slice. Careful, it’s hot. Now take a big bite. Talk about crisp. The dough was made fresh this morning and baked in a virgin olive oil. The sauce—prepared from scratch from juicy plum tomatoes picked this morning and blended with select fresh herbs from our own garden."*
* **Application Advice:** Ramp up the intensity of the five senses in your copy so your customer can clearly visualize and experience the product in their mind. Adjust which senses you emphasize based on your specific product.

### **Lesson 2: Put Your Biggest Benefit in Your Headline**

* **Rule:** Put your product’s primary benefit directly into the headline to immediately attract the target audience, as the majority of readers only glance at the top of an ad.
* **Studies & Numbers Cited:**
  * **David Ogilvy quote:** *"Unless your headline sells your product, you’ve wasted 90% of your money."*
  * **60%** of people who see an ad only read the first few words (the headline).
  * People can grasp the meaning of **5 to 6 words** in a single glance.
* **Ad Examples Shown:**
  * **Bad Lighting Store Headline:** *"We'll Help You Brighten Up Your Life & Save Big!"* (Fails because it's vague—could be for paint, window cleaning, or antidepressants).
  * **Better Lighting Store Headline:** *"Need Lamps?"* (Directly targets people looking for lamps).
  * **Bad Workshop Headline:** *"Attention Food Servers: New Workshop Teaches You the Tricks of the Trade!"*
  * **Better Workshop Headline:** *"Attention Food Servers: New Workshop Teaches You How to Boost Your Tips by 512%... Or Your Money Back."*
* **Application Advice:** 
  1. Always put your biggest benefit in the headline.
  2. To choose headline length: Shorter headlines (5–6 words) are easier to read at a glance, but well-written longer headlines can sell more products. If writing two equally effective headlines, choose the shorter one as it will likely be read by more people.

### **Lesson 3: The Psychology of Pricing**

* **Rule:** Use odd-even pricing (odd numbers like .99, .98, .95) to make prices look cheaper or on sale, or use rounded whole numbers to signal higher quality and luxury.
* **Studies & Numbers Cited:**
  * **Rutgers University Study:** A dress priced at **$49.99** was rated lower in quality compared to the exact same dress priced at **$50.00**.
* **Examples & Visuals:**
  * **Odd-Even Pricing Theory:** Prices ending in **.77**, **.95**, or **.99** (e.g., **$9.77** vs. **$10.00**; **$0.64** vs. **$0.70** for bananas) feel like cheaper options because consumers assume the seller calculated the lowest possible price.
  * **Left-Digit Effect:** Consumers mentally drop the cents (**$9.78** is perceived as **$9.00** instead of rounding up to **$10.00**).
  * **Perception of Sales:** Prices ending in **.98** or **.99** (e.g., **$599.98** vs. **$600.00**) make buyers believe the item is on sale.
  * **High Quality / Luxury Pricing:** Round whole numbers (e.g., **$1000** vs. **$999.95**) convey superior quality and are preferred by luxury brands.

### **Lesson 4: Test Different Offers**

* **Rule:** If an ad fails, don't assume customers don't want the product—test different offers, angles, or framings to see what resonates best.
* **Examples & Visuals (Chiropractor Ad):**
  * **Offer 1 (Weak):** *"Grand Opening"*
  * **Offer 2 (Better):** *"Free Spinal Evaluation"*
  * **Offer 3 (Alternative):** *"50% Off First Visit"*
  * **Offer 4 (Reframed):** *"2 Visits for the Price of 1"* (Same mathematical value as 50% off, but framed differently).
* **Application Advice:** Continually test varied offers and messaging structures to find the specific offer that drives audience response.
