### **Lesson #5: Associate with Models**
*   **The Rule:** Associating your product with people who represent your customers' aspirations/egos can persuade them to buy it.
*   **Examples:** 
    *   Marlboro Man: Associated with ego, power, self-confidence, and appeal to women.
    *   Victoria’s Secret: Associated with ego and a desire to be sexy. 
    *   Dog groomer: Show photos of rich celebrities who bring in their dogs.
    *   Security firm: Show logos of big companies who use the security firm like banks and local jewellery stores.
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Show your product with people or images that make buyers believe using it will make them more like those people. 

### **Lesson #6: The Positive End Result**
*   **The Rule:** Advertise what the product will do for the customer in the future.
*   **Examples:** 
    *   Microwave oven: More time for other things. 
    *   Lexus car: To a realtor, it looks like you are successful and selling more homes, so people will want to list their houses with you.
    *   Luxury goods and services: Provide secondary benefits.
    *   Window sign for retail business: 
        1. Tells people who you are.
        2. People will do business with you.
        3. Sell your products.
        4. Make money.
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Use writing and images that represent the positive end result of buying your product. 

### **Lesson #7: The Psychology of Color**
*   **The Rule:** Colors can affect people’s perceptions of weight, size, and taste. 
*   **Examples:**
    *   Study by psychologists on the effect of color on weight and size: People were shown 8 boxes of the same size and asked to rank them based on weight. Results showed that darker colors appeared heavier. 
        *   White - 3.1
        *   Yellow - 3.5
        *   Green - 4.1
        *   Blue - 4.7
        *   Purple - 4.8
        *   Gray - 4.8
        *   Red - 4.9
        *   Black - 5.8
    *   Food manufacturer: Switched to darker packaging so it looked like there was more food inside.
    *   Manufacturer: Switched from black boxes to light green boxes so employees felt they were lighter and easier to carry.
    *   Canada Dry sugar-free ginger ale: Changed can color from red to green and white, resulting in a 25% increase in sales because it looked more like ginger ale. 
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Choose packaging and advertising colors that match the desired perception of the product.

### **Lesson #8: Crush Your Competition With Extreme Specificity**
*   **The Rule:** Provide extreme specifics to educate your prospects about your product or service.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Hardware store #1: 
        *   Headline: Hardware Store #1
        *   Ad copy: Hammers, screwdrivers, power tools, home fix-up, lawn and garden equipment. Paulson’s has the hardware you’re looking for. At neighborhood friendly prices! 
    *   Hardware store #2: 
        *   Headline: Hardware Store #2
        *   Ad copy: Handymen Jack’s isn’t just any hardware store. It’s a super store. We have 343 kinds of fasteners, 28 types of nails, 86 kinds of wire, 43 kinds of sandpaper, 16 different hammers, 28 kinds of screwdrivers, and 47 types of keys. Plus we have 354,000 bolts and screws. The best power tools at great prices, and a money-back guarantee…! If you’re not happy. 
    *   Pizza shops: 
        *   Shop #1: “We use better ingredients. Our sauce and flour are from Italy.”
        *   Shop #2: “To make the cheese better, we use hard-to-find buffalo milk mozzarella. The cheese is never shredded, because shredded cheese releases too much moisture.”
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Be extremely specific when describing your products or services and intensify the 5 senses.
