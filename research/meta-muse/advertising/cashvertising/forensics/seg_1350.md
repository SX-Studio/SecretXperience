**Lesson 13: Transfer Credibility**
*   **The Rule:** Credibility is transferred when an offer is endorsed by a person or group respected by the buyer.
*   **Concrete Examples:** Nespresso ad (with George Clooney) with headline “Nespresso. What else?”. 
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Find a person or organization in your industry with a reputation for respect and get them to endorse your product or service. 

**Lesson 14: Bombard Your Readers with Benefits**
*   **The Rule:** Focus on benefits (how the product improves the user's life) over features (technical specifications).
*   **Concrete Examples:** 
    *   Inkjet printer (Product).
    *   Multiple ink tanks (Feature) → Save money by replacing only the empty color (Benefit).
    *   500-sheet tray (Feature) → Refill the tray less often (Benefit).
    *   Author's product: "Daytime Organizer" (mentioned in lesson 17).
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** For every product feature, ask “What’s in it for my buyers?” until you reach the core benefit.

**Lesson 15: Scarcity**
*   **The Rule:** Deadlines and scarcity create a sense of urgency that motivates buyers to act now rather than delay.
*   **Concrete Examples:** 
    *   On-screen phrases: 
        *   “Offer expires May 15th”
        *   “Supplies are strictly limited” (example: 8/10 toolboxes left)
        *   “Offer good only before 4:00 pm”
        *   “Seating is limited to 50 participants”
        *   “Discount pricing only until August 3rd”
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Create the perception of scarcity with clear, powerful deadlines.

**Lesson 16: The Psychology of “Social Proof”**
*   **The Rule:** All types of buyers believe and value testimonials from others.
*   **Concrete Examples:** A website designer asking clients specific questions to generate testimonials.
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Ask customers for their honest opinions. Use specific questions to make it easy for them to provide high-quality testimonials.

**Lesson 17: Length Implies Strength**
*   **The Rule:** Longer ads with more facts, figures, and testimonials create a stronger perception of credibility and truth.
*   **Concrete Examples:** 
    *   Brochure: “101 Success Stories!” (Headline), containing 101 testimonials and photos.
    *   Ad for author's product “Daytime Organizer” (Headline), listing 22 reasons to buy.
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** 1) Load ads with as many photos and testimonials as possible. 2) Write long, engaging sales pages. 3) List numerous reasons for prospects to buy.

**Lesson 18: Power Your Ads with Pictures**
*   **The Rule:** Visual content significantly increases the visibility, memorability, and attractiveness of an advertisement.
*   **Study/Source:** A study (source not cited) comparing ads with and without visuals.
*   **Concrete Numbers:** 
    *   Ads with 50% visuals are seen/remembered 30% more often than ads with no visuals.
    *   A photo of the product attracts readers 13% more often.
    *   A photo of the product being used increases reader attraction by an *additional* 13% (26% total).
*   **Advice on how to apply it:** Use visuals (photos, graphics, or illustrations) that comprise at least 50% of the ad. Specifically include photos showing the product in use.
