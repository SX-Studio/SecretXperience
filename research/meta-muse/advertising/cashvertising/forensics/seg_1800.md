### **Lesson 19: Show A Face**

* **Rule:** Including a head or face looking directly at the reader in your advertising attracts maximum attention and builds trust by making your business personal and human.
* **Concrete Examples, Studies, Numbers & Ads:**
  * **Tear Gas Ad Example:** Showing a close-up, screaming, distorted face of a 235-pound mugger who just got sprayed directly in the face is far more effective than a generic image.
  * **Professional Ads:**
    * **Carpenter:** Show a close-up photo of your face.
    * **Dentist:** Show your face rather than just a tooth icon to create a warm, personal feel.
  * **On-Screen Ad Example (CA$HVERTISING Seminar):**
    * Author Drew Eric Whitman placed his professional photo at the top of the ad.
    * **Headline Copy:** *"Drew Eric Whitman - Give Me 90 Minutes...!"* along with the book cover *"Cashvertising: 100 Secrets..."*
  * **Interior Designer Profile Comparison:**
    * **Candidate #1:** Out-of-focus distant shot in winter clothes outdoors.
    * **Candidate #2:** Professional, smiling headshot wearing glasses and business casual attire (clearly signals authority and credibility).
* **Exact Advice on How to Apply It:**
  1. **Hire a Professional Photographer** (or a skilled friend) to take a high-quality professional headshot dressed appropriately for your business.
  2. **Place It Everywhere:** Put your photo on all sales materials, including ads, emails, websites, sales pages, podcast interviews, webinars, online courses, books, and weekly newsletters.

### **Lesson 20: Powerful Visual Adjective (PVA)**

* **Rule:** Use descriptive, specific visual adjectives to help buyers imagine your product clearly in their minds, taking up more mental space and creating an inner "mental movie."
* **Concrete Examples, Studies, Numbers & Ads:**
  * **Restaurant Example 1 (Weak/Generic):** *"People love our authentic Italian food. Try it, it’s delicious."*
  * **Restaurant Example 2 (Strong PVA):** *"We make our pasta fresh every morning. We bake our own bread—golden and crusty. Our sauces are made from scratch, never from cans. Everything we serve is homemade, 100% natural and delicious."* (Result: Creates a clear, bright mental picture of fresh food).
  * **Before / After Copy Comparisons:**
    * **Money/Earnings:**
      * ❌ *Don't say:* "Make lots of money"
      * ✅ *Do say:* "Earn $2,750 cash weekly"
    * **Water Cooler:**
      * ❌ *Don't say:* "Drink cleaner water"
      * ✅ *Do say:* "Enjoy pure, crystal-clear, glacier-fresh water"
* **Exact Advice on How to Apply It:**
  * Avoid general claims. Use specific, vivid adjectives that evoke clear imagery (numbers, sensory details, origins) so prospects can visually project using the product.

### **Lesson 21: Be Clear, Not Clever**

* **Rule:** Prioritize direct, unambiguous language that clearly states what you are selling over "clever" metaphors or wordplay.
* **Concrete Examples, Studies, Numbers & Ads:**
  * **Headline Comparison:**
    * **Headline 1 (Clever, but unclear):** *"$250 keeps you from getting caught in a sticky web on the net."* (Features an illustration of a spiderweb on a laptop).
    * **Headline 2 (Clear):** *"Powerful web pages designed in 24 hours by famous marketing expert for $199."* (Features clear visual icons: web browser, 24h timer, $199, professional at computer).
  * **Key Study/Data Point Cited:**
    * **60% of people** who look at ads **only read the headlines** (they scan).
    * Result: Headline 1 loses at least 60% of potential buyers because they don't know what is being sold. Headline 2 immediately converts interested readers.
* **Exact Advice on How to Apply It:**
  * State your value proposition directly in the headline. Make sure anyone scanning in one second knows *what* you offer, *how much* it costs, and *why* they need it.

### **Lesson 22: Survey Power**

* **Rule:** Instead of guessing what your customers want, ask them directly via targeted surveys and build your advertising copy around their exact feedback and desires.
* **Concrete Examples, Studies, Numbers & Ads:**
  * **Key Question to Ask:** *"If you were the owner of my business, what would you do differently?"*
  * **Pizzeria Survey Example:** Ask questions like:
    * What is most important to you when going out for pizza?
    * What do you like or hate?
    * Would you come in more often if offered 2 free drinks with every large pizza?
    * What's your biggest complaint?
  * **Incentive / Urgency Strategy:**
    * "Bribe" prospects to complete the survey by offering a reward (e.g., *"FREE slice as a thank you"*).
    * Apply the **Scarcity Factor** by adding a strict deadline date (e.g., *"20/25"*). Without a deadline, people procrastinate and never respond.
* **Exact Advice on How to Apply It:**
  * Send the survey to everyone within your local service area (e.g., 5-mile radius) and existing customer lists. Use their answers to formulate your future marketing hooks and offers.

### **Lesson 23: Compare With Competitors**

* **Rule:** Help prospective buyers choose you over competitors by explicitly contrasting your superior quality and service standards against their hidden flaws.
* **Concrete Examples, Studies, Numbers & Ads:**
  * **Pauley's Pizza Comparison Example:**
    * **Mozzarella Comparison:**
      * *Competitor:* Claims to use "fresh mozzarella," but buys it pre-shredded in big plastic bags.
      * *Pauley's:* "At Pauley's Pizza, we hand-shred our mozzarella every morning."
    * **Delivery Speed Comparison:**
      * *Competitor:* Promotes "convenient delivery," but average delivery time is over 1 hour (60 min).
      * *Pauley's:* "Pauley's Pizza delivers in 28 minutes or your pizza is free!"
* **Exact Advice on How to Apply It:**
  * Frame the facts through **your lens**. Point out what buyers should look out for in your industry (what's good vs. bad) and provide concrete facts proving why your offering is superior.
