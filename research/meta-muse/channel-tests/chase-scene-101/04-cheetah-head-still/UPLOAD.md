Slowed down 60 times, a cheetah sprint reveals something strange: the body thrashes, but the head barely moves. In this footage the hips move about 11 times more than the head (measured by tracking the video).

Its spine coils and springs to stretch each stride to about 7 meters. Twice in every stride, all four feet leave the ground. Its claws never fully retract, so they grip like running spikes, and its long tail works like a rudder. And an unusually large balance organ in its inner ear keeps its eyes locked on the target.
