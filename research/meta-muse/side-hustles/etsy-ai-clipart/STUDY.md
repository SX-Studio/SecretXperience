# Study: Dubibubi, "I Asked AI To Make Me As Much Money As Possible In 10 Days (RAW RESULTS)"

Video: https://youtu.be/xEKaDesfQK0 (23:12). Studied 2026-10-22 with Gemini, in six 4-minute segments. The raw notes are in `forensics/`.

## What he did

An AI agent controlled his Mac and ran an Etsy shop of AI-generated digital clipart. The shop was "LittleWonderStudioAU", selling watercolour PNGs. The agent was ChatGPT's computer-use / Codex agent, which he calls "Astra".

He set three rules:
1. The AI does the work.
2. He checks in once a day.
3. No outside promotion, so every sale is organic.

The agent named the shop, made the logo and banner, generated the images, wrote the listings and uploaded them. On day 2 it began choosing themes with the keyword tool eRank, and it ran an hourly automation that made three listings an hour.

## Results, from his own Etsy dashboard

| Day | Listings | Views / visits | Orders | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 18 | 14 / 7 | 0 | AU$0 |
| 3 | 53 | 37 / 18 | 0 | AU$0 |
| 6 | 187 | about 33 visits (30 days) | 0 | AU$0 |
| **10** | **191** | **48 visits** | **2** | **AU$4.55** |

He also had 8 abandoned baskets, worth up to AU$28. He didn't send them discount offers.

## The money

- **Listing fees:** 191 listings × AU$0.29 = **about AU$55**.
- **Etsy's cut of each sale:** 6.5% transaction fee, plus 3.4% + AU$0.25 payment processing. Offsite Ads take 15% when they bring the sale, and currency conversion adds 2.5%.
- **Other costs:** a one-time shop setup fee, and his ChatGPT plan. The first listing alone used 4% of his weekly agent allowance; optimizing brought that down to 0.33%.
- **Net:** after 10 days the shop is **about AU$55–70 down**, before counting the AI subscription. He says himself it needs "a proper look at the costs before calling this a business that pays for itself".
- **His earlier claim:** before AI, he says, he made $600–1,000 a month on Etsy (about $10k in total). That shop sold **Harry Potter stickers without a licence and was permanently banned**, so that income isn't a fair benchmark for this one.

## What actually works in it (the useful part)

- **Keyword research before making anything.** In eRank, "christmas png" had 11.3k searches against 843k competing listings, while "mahjong png" had 1.1k searches against only 642. The opportunities are in small niches.
- **Bundles and sales.** "Every single store on Etsy does the exact same tactics … bundles and … sales." His second order came after he added bundles and discounts.
- **Reviews.** "That first sale is really important … if they leave a good review."
- **Abandoned-basket offers.** He missed 8 of these.
- **His own conclusion:** "I started treating the numbers of listings as the scoreboard, but I still needed to give someone a reason to buy."

## Risks

- **Saturation.** Generic AI clipart is the most crowded category on Etsy, and a new shop gets very little traffic.
- **Etsy policy.** Etsy's creativity standards require sellers to disclose AI-generated designs. Unlicensed IP gets a shop banned, as it did with his earlier one.
- **Automation.** Hundreds of listings made by a bot are the kind of pattern that triggers Trust & Safety reviews.
- **Slow ramp.** Etsy search rewards sales history and reviews, which take weeks to months to build.

## Verdict

As shown, this is a loss so far: AU$4.55 in revenue against about AU$55 in listing fees. It can still become a small side income if three things change:
- **Fewer, better listings.** About 30 instead of 191.
- **Niche keywords chosen first**, with fewer than about 2k competing listings and real search volume.
- **Bundles with a commercial licence**, timed to the season. Christmas demand peaks November to mid-December, so the window is now.

Expect pocket money in the first months, not a main income. The best use is as a by-product of art we already generate for the video channels. Never sell art based on franchises.
