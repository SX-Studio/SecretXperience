# Social Ripple: content map ("cover all angles")

**Mission (the user's words, 2026-10-31):** perfectly depict and describe, through visual experiences, how we collectively affect and constantly shape our shared experiences. Butterfly effects, and every other angle.

**The one line every film proves:** *nothing you do stays with you.*

## The 9 angles (content pillars)

| # | Angle | What it covers | Signature picture (engine) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Person to person** | Moods, kindness, rudeness, habits and health passing between people | Ripple rings and a thread jumping head to head; degree counter 1°, 2°, 3° |
| 2 | **Crowds and emergence** | Patterns nobody planned: traffic jams from nothing, stadium waves, panic buying, applause | 100+ stick figures as a crowd; the pattern drawn over them in gold or red |
| 3 | **Butterfly effects in history** | One tiny moment that changed millions of lives | An "archive" palette (sepia sky, ink map); a ripple spreading across a map |
| 4 | **Systems and side effects** | Fixes that backfire: cobra effect, Streisand effect, rebound effects | A loop diagram that grows one arrow per beat; the fix turning red |
| 5 | **Ideas and information** | Rumours, misinformation, trends, memes, social media | A phone screen as a world; shares as threads branching into a tree |
| 6 | **Shared things** | Commons, herd immunity, norms, litter, voting, trust | One shared object (a park, a well, a bus) worn down or rebuilt by everyone |
| 7 | **Across time** | Generations, teachers, habits handed down, trees planted for strangers | Time stamps jumping years; the same place aging; a child becoming the parent |
| 8 | **Nature and the planet** | The original butterfly effect (weather), wolves and rivers, tipping points | Landscape sets; animals in the same flat style; rings on water |
| 9 | **Inside you** | How the crowd shapes the self: conformity, norms, language, taste | The inside of a head as a room full of the people you've met |

Every film still ends the same way. The camera **pulls back until the people become dots and the threads become a web** (the ripple map), then the mantra card appears. **The butterfly** hides somewhere in every film, which gives viewers a "Spot the butterfly" game in the comments.

## Series (formats)

- **Three Degrees** (flagship, long-form 3–5 min). One act travels through strangers and comes back. Angles 1, 6 and 7.
- **The Butterfly Files** (long-form 4–6 min). A real moment in history: the small thing, the chain, and what if it hadn't happened. Angle 3.
- **What If Everyone…** (long-form 3–5 min). A collective what-if, built on the same search demand that took Ifverse to 4.6M views. Angles 2, 4 and 6.
- **Invisible Chains** (long-form). Every person behind one ordinary thing, such as your morning coffee. Angles 6 and 7.
- **Ripple Shorts** (45–60 s, 9:16). One mechanism, one picture, one number, plus a vertical cut of each long-form.

## 40 episode ideas

Status: ✔ = the source has been checked; ☐ = verify against the paper before scripting. Disputed findings are named as disputed in the film.

**Person to person**
1. ✔ *The Bad Mood That Reached a Stranger.* Rudeness contagion (Foulk et al. 2016) plus three degrees of happiness (Fowler & Christakis, BMJ 2008: 15.3 / 9.8 / 5.6%). **Episode 1.**
2. ✔ *The Generosity That Tripled.* Each act of giving was tripled by the people it reached (Fowler & Christakis, PNAS 2010).
3. ☐ *Your Friend's Friend Decides Your Weight.* Obesity spreading in networks (Christakis & Fowler, NEJM 2007); also disputed.
4. ☐ *Why Quitting Smoking Happens in Groups.* Clusters of people quit together (Christakis & Fowler, NEJM 2008).
5. ☐ *They Liked You More Than You Think.* The liking gap (Boothby et al. 2018).
6. ☐ *The Kindness You Almost Didn't Do.* People underestimate how much a small kindness is valued (Kumar & Epley 2022).
7. ☐ *Why You Just Yawned.* Contagious yawning, empathy and closeness.

**Crowds and emergence**

8. ☐ *The Traffic Jam With No Cause.* 22 cars on a circle and a jam appears from nothing (Sugiyama et al. 2008).
9. ☐ *How 25 People Start a Stadium Wave.* The Mexican wave (Farkas, Helbing & Vicsek, Nature 2002).
10. ☐ *The Toilet Paper Panic of 2020.* Panic buying as a self-fulfilling prophecy.
11. ☐ *How a Bank Dies in a Day.* A bank run, e.g. SVB 2023.
12. ☐ *Nobody Goes First.* Pluralistic ignorance (Latané & Darley 1968).
13. ☐ *The Riot That Nobody Wanted.* Threshold models (Granovetter 1978).
14. ☐ *How Mild Preferences Build Divided Cities.* The Schelling model (1971).

**Butterfly effects in history**

15. ☐ *The Wrong Turn That Started a World War.* Franz Ferdinand's driver, Sarajevo 1914.
16. ☐ *The Man Who Didn't Press the Button.* Stanislav Petrov, 1983.
17. ☐ *The Vote That Stopped a Nuclear Torpedo.* Vasili Arkhipov, 1962.
18. ☐ *The Press Conference Mistake That Opened the Berlin Wall.* Günter Schabowski, 1989.
19. ☐ *The City Saved by a Memory.* Kyoto removed from the target list (Stimson; the "honeymoon" story is disputed, and the film says so).
20. ☐ *The Weatherman Who Found the Butterfly.* Lorenz, 1961–72, the rounding error that named the effect.

**Systems and side effects**

21. ☐ *The Rat Bounty That Bred More Rats.* Hanoi 1902 (Michael Vann), better documented than the cobra story.
22. ☐ *Trying to Hide It Made Millions See It.* The Streisand effect, 2003.
23. ☐ *Why Wider Roads Mean More Traffic.* Induced demand.
24. ☐ *The Safety Feature That Made People Reckless.* Risk compensation; disputed.

**Ideas and information**

25. ☐ *Why a Lie Travels Faster Than the Truth.* False news was 70% more likely to be retweeted (Vosoughi, Roy & Aral, Science 2018).
26. ☐ *One Post Changed 689,000 Moods.* The Facebook emotional contagion study (Kramer et al., PNAS 2014), and the ethics row it caused.
27. ☐ *How a Rumour Mutates.* The telephone game, and serial reproduction (Bartlett 1932).
28. ☐ *How a Word Is Born.* Slang spreading through networks.

**Shared things**

29. ☐ *The First Piece of Litter.* Litter attracts litter (Cialdini et al. 1990; Keizer et al., Science 2008).
30. ☐ *Why 95% Matters.* Herd immunity, with measles as the worked example.
31. ☐ *The Field That Everyone Ruined.* The tragedy of the commons (Hardin 1968), and how communities avoid it (Ostrom, Nobel 2009).
32. ☐ *The Path Nobody Designed.* Desire paths.

**Across time**

33. ☐ *The Teacher Who Changed Your Paycheck.* Teacher value-added and lifetime earnings (Chetty et al. 2014).
34. ☐ *The Habit Your Grandmother Gave You.* Intergenerational transmission.
35. ☐ *The Tree Planted for Someone Not Yet Born.*
36. ☐ *The Compliment That Lasted 10 Years.*

**Nature and the planet**

37. ☐ *How Wolves Changed Yellowstone.* The 1995 trophic cascade; the size of the effect is debated.
38. ☐ *Why One Degree Changes Everything.* Climate tipping points.

**Inside you**

39. ☐ *Why You Agreed With the Wrong Answer.* Asch conformity (1951).
40. ☐ *The Butterfly Effect Isn't What You Think.* A myth-check: chaos means sensitivity, not small causes reliably making big effects.

## The other angles (how the channel itself ripples)

- **Truth.** Every number appears on screen with its source and is checked against the paper. Disputed findings are labelled as disputed.
- **Tone.** The red half of every film is answered by a gold half. The channel shows harm spreading, but it never ends on fear.
- **Search.** Titles follow the rules in `../advertising/cashvertising/` (the benefit or stakes in under 6 words, clear not clever) and the proven "What if…" pattern.
- **Comments.** Every film ends on a question ("Who's in your three degrees?"), plus the "Spot the butterfly" game. Viewers' own ripple stories can become episodes, with their written permission and changed names.
- **Languages.** Kokoro has voices in Spanish, French, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese and Chinese. Because the visuals are wordless, the films could be dubbed using YouTube's multi-language audio where the channel has it. Native-speaker review is still needed.
- **Shorts to long-form.** Each Short ends on the long-form's question and links to it as the related video.
- **Revenue beyond ads.** Schools teaching social-emotional learning, licensing, and sponsors that fit (wellbeing, education). This only starts once the channel has an audience.
