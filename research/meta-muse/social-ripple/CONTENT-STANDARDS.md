# Social Ripple: Content Standards (absolute)

Every episode, Short, cover, title, description and comment prompt must meet these standards. Nothing is published until it passes the checklist at the bottom.

## 1. Mission
**Food for the thought the viewer already has, guiding them correctly.**
Working assumption: the algorithm shows people what they're thinking about. Every piece replies to a thought the viewer already has and leaves them with a better one.

## 2. The arc (every episode and every Short)
1. **Meet the thought:** in the viewer's inner voice ("Why do I always quit?").
2. **Mirror it in the anime:** a moment where a character they love faces the same thought, with that character on screen, so they're not alone with it.
3. **Reframe with real evidence:** what the research actually shows, limits included.
4. **Land the core truth:** one sentence per lesson, given its own moment.
5. **Hand them a better next thought:** one small, doable step.
6. **Let them answer:** a comment prompt inviting their version of the thought.

## 3. Depth: what matters most
- Before writing, state each lesson's **core truth in one sentence**, and why it matters to the viewer's life. Every scene serves that sentence.
- **Fewer lessons, deeper:** three that land beat five that skim. Each gets its "so what".
- The core line gets **its own moment:** poster card, a beat of silence, the character feeling it.
- **End each lesson on the truth, not the statistic.** Close the episode on one sentence that ties it together.

## 4. Topics and words
- Start from a **question people already ask themselves**. Sources: YouTube search autocomplete ("why do I…", "how to stop…"), comments under anime clips, our own comments.
- Then find the **popular anime moment** that answers it (Naruto, One Piece, Solo Leveling, …).
- **Titles** in the viewer's inner voice, plus the anime name for search.
- **Captions** highlight the words they'd use in their own head.

## 5. Covers and first frames
- Formula: **relatable situation + body sensation (a felt "ouch") + mystery + a specific number**.
- The anime character sits *inside* an everyday moment the viewer has lived (not just a pose), with the anime name in big text.
- **A Short's first frame = its cover:** fully drawn, big text, no blank paper.

## 6. Camera dynamics: stimulated from start to finish
- **The frame is never still.** Every shot slowly pushes in, with gentle drift and sway (built into the engine).
- **No shot longer than ~4 seconds** without a new camera move, cut, or reveal.
- **Punch-in on every key word and number:** a quick zoom toward the word or face as it's said.
- **Push in on the face for the core truth:** the emotional line lands close on the character.
- **Shake on impacts:** poster slams, hits, big reveals.
- **Vary the transitions:** glide, whip-pan, zoom-through, pull-out reveal, poster cut. Never the same move twice in a row.
- **Match the energy:** fast and punchy for hooks and battles; slower, closer and quieter for the core truth.
- **Shorts:** a move every 1.5–2.5 seconds.

## 6b. Characters: who appears when
- **Anime characters when the lesson references them.** When the narration is telling an anime moment, that anime's characters are on screen, drawn and animated in our own style with accurate signature details (hair, eyes, outfit, marks, props, and canon touches such as Naruto's goggles before he earns the headband). Check every detail against references first; a fan must never think "that doesn't look like him".
- **Continuity is absolute.** Each anime character has one locked model sheet (saved in `cast/`) that matches canon: correct outfit, colours, accessories and marks for the era or scene being referenced (for example Haku's hunter-nin mask, green haori and bun holder; Naruto's goggles before the headband). The same design is reused in every episode, Short and cover. Check the model sheet against references before first use, and never improvise colours or outfits.
- **The Social Ripple crew for everything else:** hosting, the viewer's own situations, study explanations, conclusions, summaries, the small step and the outro. Nia, Kai, Mo and Rue have common hairstyles and current streetwear so viewers see themselves in them.
- **Story-specific extras** are created per episode as needed.
- **All drawn by us:** no official art, screenshots, footage, logos or soundtrack. Plot is told in our own words; no dialogue quoted beyond a few words.
- **Rigged and always alive:** breathing, blinking, mouths that move with the voice, side view for walking and floor moves, correct anatomy (hands plant, joints bend the right way).
- **Motion correlates with the narration:** characters act out what's said, react to on-screen events and shift expression on emotional lines; camera and character move together.

## 6c. Highlight the anime characters (fan moments)
Some viewers come for the anime first, so every episode and Short gives them a reason to stay:
- **An iconic line on screen** at the moment it belongs to, a few words at most ("OF COURSE I WILL!", "SAY IT!", "I want to live!", Luffy's "shishishi").
- **A signature pose or moment**, acted by the rig (Luffy's fists in the air, the hat placed on Nami's head, Sogeking's shot burning the flag).
- **A name plate** with the character's name or epithet on first appearance ("STRAW HAT LUFFY", "CAT BURGLAR NAMI", Nico Robin "the Devil Child").
- **A burst behind the character** in the opening so they stand out from the paper.
- Everything is checked against the source first (line, episode, outfit); a wrong catchphrase is worse than none. Lines stay short and are never the lesson itself: the lesson still comes from the research and the crew.

## 7. Shorts
- Hook line under 2 seconds; 20–30 seconds; one idea; last line loops into the first.
- The referenced anime character in every Short, plus the anime name on screen; each Short voices one thought, and the long video is the full answer.
- **Opening under test (from 2026-11-28).** Every Short so far has had under 50% "stayed to watch", across different topics, so the shared opening is the suspect. The test format for the first two seconds:
  - the cover look stays: black headline slab, white and red text, yellow anime tag (the channel owner likes this style; keep it);
  - under the slab the scene fills the frame, with the anime character in close-up and a burst behind them;
  - the viewer's own thought as a question of four words or fewer, on screen and spoken, with the voice starting on the first frame ("Can't ask for help?");
  - something moving from frame 0 (a hat placed, a head dropping, a hand raised to knock), and a sound hit on the headline;
  - the anime name in the first two seconds;
  - bold two-word captions over the scene;
  - a character highlight in the first half (see 6c).
  This is a hypothesis, not a finding. Judge it on Studio's "viewed vs. swiped away" for the One Piece Shorts against the earlier ones, then keep it, change it or drop it.
- **Shorts rulebook (YouTube Studio guidance, 2026-11-28).** Baseline: Shorts average 44.7% "viewed vs. swiped away" (habit Short 45.8%, ice-water Short 43.5%). Target 75%+, which Studio calls ambitious. Every new Short follows these:
  1. **No warm-up.** Start mid-action on the provocative line. No scene-setting, greeting or slow draw-on.
  2. **Audio and picture say the same thing at frame zero.** The on-screen hook is the spoken hook, three to five words, large, high contrast, centred.
  3. **Lead with tension about the viewer**, as a question or a belief turned upside down, not with background ("Someone is better than you.", not "In Dragon Ball Z, Vegeta...").
  4. **Open loop.** In the first five seconds raise a question that is only answered in the final seconds. If the answer arrives at second ten, they leave at second ten.
  5. **A visual change every 2 to 3 seconds:** character cutaway, moving key-word text, punch-in or angle change.
  6. **Trim every breath.** Gaps between narration lines of about 0.12 s, voice a little faster, no trailing silence.
  7. **No exit signals.** No "so", "that's why", "in conclusion", "remember". Deliver the punchline and end.
  8. **Seamless loop.** The last sentence runs grammatically into the first one, with no bridge phrase, and the picture is back on the first frame.
- **Improve forward, don't redo.** Lessons from each upload go into the next piece. Published or delivered content is not re-cut, re-rendered or re-issued unless the channel owner asks for that specific piece (owner's instruction, 2026-11-28).

## 8. Truth and care (guardrails)
- **Every claim checked against the original paper** (or the best primary source available), with limits stated honestly; never more than a study found. Anime plot facts checked too (episode numbers).
- **No shame, no fear-bait:** covers can carry a pain point, but every video ends better than it started.
- **Our suggestions are labelled as ours**, never presented as findings.
- **Heavy topics** (loneliness, regret, grief, self-worth): a gentle "talk to someone you trust" line in the video, plus a support resource in the description.

## 9. Look, sound and rights
- **Everything drawn by us:** ink-notebook style, poster cards, the Social Ripple crew, and our own drawings of the anime characters. No official art, screenshots, footage, logos or soundtrack.
- Narration: Nia (Kokoro af_heart). Music and sound: original. Loudness −14 LUFS. Captions on screen.
- Credit the anime's creators in the description; it's commentary with our own drawings.

## 10. Delivery
- Upload info (title, alternatives, description with sources and chapters, tags, settings, pinned comment) **in chat**.
- Everything saved and pushed to the repo.
