# Pilot storylines (pick one; A is recommended)

All three are 16:9 at about 3–4 minutes, with a 45–60 s vertical Short cut from each. Each is built with `engine/sr-engine.js`, an original ACE-Step score, and Foley on visible actions.

## A. "Three Degrees" (recommended)

**Device and form:** a chain of strangers, told as an object-to-object chain. Every shot ends on the person who opens the next one, joined by a ripple-ring wipe.

**The story.** At 7:42 AM a stressed driver, Dev, honks at Maya at a crosswalk. Maya snaps at Leo the barista. Leo is short with Sam, a nine-year-old buying a hot chocolate. The red ripple grows with every jump, and a **degree counter** ticks 1°, 2°, 3°.

Then Sam does the opposite. Sam holds the door for a struggling old man and leaves a doodle on Leo's cup. The gold ripple runs back up the chain:
1. Leo laughs and gives Maya her coffee free.
2. Maya waves a car through at the crossing.
3. It's Dev, who was late because his daughter was in hospital. He doesn't honk.

The loop closes on the crossing where the film began.

- **First 2 seconds:** a close-up of a hand slamming a car horn, with "7:42 AM" stamped in the corner and a red ring bursting from the horn.
- **Beats:** honk → Maya snaps → Leo snaps → Sam's face falls → **phenomenon card**: "RUDENESS IS CONTAGIOUS. People who received rudeness passed it on, even a week later. (Foulk et al., 2016)" → Sam's tiny kindness → gold ripple back up the chain → **study card**: "15.3% · 9.8% · 5.6%: how much more likely you are to be happy when someone 1, 2 or 3 steps away is (one 20-year study, BMJ 2008)" → Dev doesn't honk → **mantra**: "You'll never meet most of the people your mood reaches." → question: "Who's in your three degrees?"
- **Look:** morning street, café, bus stop, hospital corridor in flashback; red and gold are the only saturated colours.
- **Sound:** a ticking pulse that tightens with each red degree, cut to silence on the turn. Warm piano and plucked strings rise with the gold return. Foley: horn, café bell, cup, door.
- **Voice:** a calm narrator for about 35% of the film, plus a few speech bubbles.
- **Why it's recommended:** everyone has had this morning. It shows the channel's whole device in one film (out, turn, back), so the first episode is the channel trailer.

## B. "The Little Storm" (the bold one)

**Device and form:** a character does the work, in one continuous take. The bad mood is **a small red storm cloud with eyes** that hops from head to head. The camera never cuts: it follows the cloud along one long street, through a bus and into a school.

**The story.** A girl, Ana, refuses to catch it. She answers it with a joke, and the cloud shrinks into a raindrop.

- **Language:** no narrator; speech bubbles and two cards only, so it plays in every language.
- **First 2 seconds:** a tiny cloud pops out of a slammed office door and looks around for the next head.
- **Look:** one continuous painted street at noon; the cloud is the only red thing.
- **Sound:** pizzicato strings that follow the cloud's hops; a thunder crack each time it lands; a kid's laugh at the turn.
- **Risk:** a cute mascot can read as aimed at kids. Older viewers may click less, but Shorts would likely do well.

## C. "Nobody Goes First"

**Device and form:** a moment in someone's day, built as one frame that rebuilds: a lunchroom seen from the same wide angle every day.

**The story.**
1. A new kid, Theo, eats alone for 9 days, and a day counter ticks.
2. Everyone notices; nobody moves, because nobody else is moving.
3. On day 10 one kid, June, sits down.
4. The next day four more do.
5. Twenty years later Theo is the one standing up at a work meeting.

- **Phenomenon card:** "PLURALISTIC IGNORANCE. Alone, 75% of people reported a room filling with smoke. With two calm strangers beside them, 10% did. (Latané & Darley, 1968)." These figures will be checked against the paper before the card is set.
- **Look:** one warm lunchroom, with the light changing by day and season.
- **Sound:** cafeteria ambience that thins as Theo's isolation grows; one piano note when June sits; the room's noise swells back.
- **Voice:** grown-up Theo narrates in the past tense.
