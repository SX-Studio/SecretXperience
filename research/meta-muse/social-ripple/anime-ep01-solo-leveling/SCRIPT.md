# Solo Leveling ep: script (narration lines by chapter id)

**a1** The weakest hunter in the world became the strongest.

**a2** And the first thing his new power asked of him wasn't a battle, or a secret technique. It was push ups.

**a3** Every single day.

**a4** Solo Leveling is a story about leveling up. But underneath the shadows and the boss fights, it's really a story about habits.

**a5** And psychologists have actually studied how habits form. Some of what they found would make Jinwoo's system proud. Some of it might surprise you.

**b1** When we meet Sung Jinwoo, he's an E rank hunter. People call him the weakest hunter of all mankind.

**b2** He isn't fighting for glory. His mother is in a coma in the hospital, and he's the one looking after his younger sister. Every dangerous job is about paying the bills.

**b3** Then comes the double dungeon. A temple full of giant statues, strange rules, and almost no way out.

**b4** Jinwoo stays behind so the others can escape. And right at the edge of death, something offers him a choice. He becomes a player.

**b5** He wakes up in the hospital, and a glowing window appears in front of him. A daily quest.

**b6** One hundred push ups. One hundred sit ups. One hundred squats. And a ten kilometer run.

**b7** The first time, he ignores it. And the system drags him into the penalty zone, a desert where he has to survive giant centipedes for four hours.

**b8** So he does the quest. And when it's done, he's rewarded. Full recovery, and three stat points to spend.

**b9** And here's the part that matters. He keeps doing it. Day after day. In one episode, he even does it twice in a single day.

**c1** Lesson one. It takes longer than you think.

**c2** You've probably heard that it takes twenty one days to form a habit.

**c3** That number comes from a 1960 self help book. A plastic surgeon wrote that it usually takes a minimum of about twenty one days for a person's mental picture of themselves to change, based on what he saw in his patients.

**c4** Over the years, minimum of about twenty one days turned into, twenty one days to form a habit.

**c5** In 2010, researchers in London actually tested it. Volunteers picked one simple new habit, like drinking a glass of water with lunch, and reported every day how automatic it felt.

**c6** For the people whose data fit the researchers' model well, the middle point was sixty six days.

**c7** But the range was huge. Some got there in eighteen days. For others, the estimate stretched to two hundred and fifty four.

**c8** And exercise habits tended to take longer than eating or drinking ones.

**c9** So if you're three weeks into something and it still feels like work, you're not failing. You're on schedule.

**d1** Lesson two. Decide the when, not just the what.

**d2** Jinwoo never wakes up wondering whether he'll train today. The quest just appears. Same task, every day.

**d3** Psychologists have a name for building that kind of trigger yourself. An implementation intention. It's a plan in the form: if this happens, then I'll do that.

**d4** Like, if I finish lunch, then I'll go for a ten minute walk.

**d5** A review that combined ninety four tests, with more than eight thousand people, found that these if then plans had a medium to large effect on actually reaching goals.

**d6** And Jinwoo stacks his habits too. In one episode, he does his daily run on the way to a raid.

**d7** The point isn't motivation. It's that the decision is already made before the moment arrives.

**e1** Lesson three. Make progress visible.

**e2** The best thing about Jinwoo's system isn't the quest. It's the numbers. Strength goes up. Stats go up. He can see himself getting stronger.

**e3** Researchers Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer collected nearly twelve thousand daily diary entries from two hundred and thirty eight professionals.

**e4** When they looked at people's best days at work, the most common event was simple. They'd made progress on something meaningful. Even a small step.

**e5** That finding comes from their book and articles, and it's about work, not habits. But the idea travels well.

**e6** You don't have a status window. So make one. A calendar with an X for every day you showed up. A note on your phone. Anything that lets you see the streak grow.

**f1** Lesson four. Missing a day is not the end.

**f2** Later in season one, Jinwoo forgets his daily quest, right before his hardest fight.

**f3** In the middle of that battle, the system pulls him into the penalty zone. And that detour is what gives him the chance to recover, come back stronger, and win.

**f4** Real life is less dramatic. But the London habit study found something just as useful. Missing a single day did not materially affect how the habit formed.

**f5** What hurts isn't the miss. It's the story you tell yourself after it. I already broke it, so what's the point.

**f6** One missed day is a penalty zone. Survive it, and go back to the quest tomorrow.

**g1** So how much of your life is made of habits?

**g2** In one diary study, between a third and a half of what students did were things they did almost every day, usually in the same place.

**g3** That's the real lesson of Solo Leveling. The day Jinwoo says, arise, didn't come out of nowhere. It was built out of hundreds of ordinary mornings nobody saw.

**g4** So here's your daily quest. Pick one small thing. Decide exactly when you'll do it. Track it. And if you miss a day, come back the next one.

**g5** Give it sixty six days. Then tell me how it went.

**g6** Comment your daily quest below. And which anime should we level up next?

**g7** This is Social Ripple. See you next time.
