# One Piece episode: "Why is it so hard to ask for help?" script

Question the viewer is already asking: "Why can't I just ask for help?" Host: Nia. One Piece characters appear whenever the story references them (Nami, Luffy, Zoro, Usopp, Sanji at Arlong Park; Robin, Luffy, Sogeking at Enies Lobby); the Social Ripple crew carries the studies, the advice and the close. The story is told in our own words. Facts and sources: `RESEARCH.md`.

Core truths: L1 "The no you're afraid of is mostly in your head." · L2 "Asking for help isn't taking something. It's letting someone show up for you." · L3 "The door is usually open. But someone has to knock." · Whole: "Nami didn't get weaker when she asked. That was the moment she stopped fighting alone."

## Chapter 1: the thought (crew)
- **u1** You're drowning. And the last thing you want to do is ask anyone for help.
- **u2** Maybe you don't want to be a burden. Maybe you think they'll say no. Maybe you've carried it alone for so long, you don't know another way.
- **u3** One Piece has two of the most famous scenes in anime about exactly this. And the research on asking for help says we've got it backwards.
- **u4** Today: why asking for help is so hard. And why you should do it anyway.

## Chapter 2: Nami's story (One Piece characters)
- **v1** Nami's story starts with a deal. When she was ten, the pirate Arlong killed her adoptive mother, and took over her village.
- **v2** So Nami made him an offer. She would work for him, and buy the village back, for one hundred million berries.
- **v3** For eight years, she stole, saved, and carried it alone. She told her sister she would fight by herself, because asking anyone for help would get people killed.
- **v4** She was seven million short, when Arlong had a corrupt Marine take all of it.
- **v5** In episode thirty seven, everything she'd held together breaks. In despair, she turns on the tattoo that marks her as one of his crew. Until Luffy catches her arm.
- **v6** She yells at him. Tells him he doesn't know anything. He stays.
- **v7** And then, for the first time in eight years, she says it. Luffy. Help me.
- **v8** He puts his treasured straw hat on her head, walks a few steps, and shouts that of course he will.
- **v9** Zoro, Usopp and Sanji are already waiting. And the four of them walk to Arlong Park.

## Chapter 3: Lesson 1 (Nami, Luffy, then Kai)
- **w1** Lesson one. People want to help, far more than you think.
- **w2** Notice something. Luffy never even heard her story. He didn't need the reasons. She asked, and that was enough.
- **w3** Real people are more like that than we expect. In one study, students guessed they would need to ask about twenty strangers, before five agreed to fill out a survey. It took about ten.
- **w4** Across studies like that, with more than fourteen thousand strangers asked, people underestimated the chance of a yes by about half.
- **w5** These were small favors, mostly between strangers. But the pattern is steady. We expect a no. We usually get a yes.
- **w6** The no you're afraid of is mostly in your head.

## Chapter 4: Lesson 2 (Robin, Enies Lobby, then Rue and Mo)
- **x1** Lesson two. You are not the burden you think you are.
- **x2** Nico Robin had been running for twenty years. Her home island was destroyed when she was eight. Again and again, people who took her in sold her out.
- **x3** So when a government agency threatened her crew, she made a deal. She would give herself up, if her friends could leave safely.
- **x4** Her deepest fear, she says, was becoming a burden, and being abandoned again.
- **x5** In episode two seventy eight, her crew stands on a rooftop across from her. Luffy has their sniper burn the world government's flag. Then he shouts at her, to say she wants to live.
- **x6** And she does. She asks them to take her out to sea with them.
- **x7** Here's what research says about being a burden. People who had to ask a stranger to take their photo guessed how the stranger would feel. The strangers were more willing, happier to help, and far less bothered than expected.
- **x8** Asking for help isn't taking something. It's letting someone show up for you.

## Chapter 5: Lesson 3 (Nami, Robin, then crew)
- **y1** Lesson three. They can't see how hard it is to ask.
- **y2** Nami's whole village knew what she was doing. Robin's crew had come all that way for her. But in the end, both of them had to say the words.
- **y3** In another study, student advisors expected about thirteen classmates to come to them for help. Fewer than eight did.
- **y4** People who offer help underestimate how awkward it feels to ask. So they wait. And you wait. And nothing happens.
- **y5** The door is usually open. But someone has to knock.

## Chapter 6: what to do, care line, close (crew)
- **z1** So here's what I'd suggest. This part is ours, not the studies'.
- **z2** One. Make it small and specific. Not, I need help with everything. Just, can you look at this with me for ten minutes?
- **z3** Two. Ask one person, not the room. A direct ask is much harder to miss than a hint.
- **z4** Three. Let them decide if it's too much. That's their call to make, not yours.
- **z5** And if what you're carrying is heavy, the kind of thing Nami and Robin were carrying, please talk to someone you trust, or use one of the free support lines in the description.
- **z6** Nami didn't get weaker when she asked. That was the moment she stopped fighting alone.
