You're drowning. And the last thing you want to do is ask anyone for help.

Nami carried her village alone for eight years. Robin tried to give herself up so she wouldn't be a burden. Here's what their two most famous scenes, and real research, teach us about asking for help, and how to make it easier.

If what you're carrying is heavy, please talk to someone you trust. Free, confidential support:
• Find a helpline in your country: https://findahelpline.com
• US: call or text 988, or chat at https://chat.988lifeline.org (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 24/7, also for emotional distress)

Research in this video:
• Flynn & Lake (2008). If you need help, just ask: Underestimating compliance with direct requests for help. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. (Students guessed they'd need to ask about 20 strangers to get 5 surveys filled in; it took about 10. Small favors, strangers, small samples.)
• Bohns (2016). (Mis)understanding our influence over others. Current Directions in Psychological Science. (Review: more than 14,000 strangers asked; people underestimated the chance of a yes by 48% on average. Mostly strangers, face to face.)
• Zhao & Epley (2022). Surprisingly happy to have helped. Psychological Science. (Visitors to a public garden asked a stranger to take their photo; strangers were more willing, happier to help and less inconvenienced than askers expected. Small, quick favors.)
• Bohns & Flynn (2010). "Why didn't you just ask?" Underestimating the discomfort of help-seeking. Journal of Experimental Social Psychology. (Peer advisors expected about 13 students to come for help; fewer than 8 did.)

The three steps at the end are our suggestions, not study findings. One Piece is by Eiichiro Oda, anime by Toei Animation (the scenes discussed are from episodes 37 and 278). This is an original animated commentary: the story is told in our own words with our own drawings (no footage or official art). Music and sound: original.
