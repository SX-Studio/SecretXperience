Dragon Ball Z characters appear whenever the story references them, in the outfit of the era being told (see `../cast/dbz-characters-sheet.jpg` and `RESEARCH.md` A8); the crew carries the studies, the advice and the close. The story is told in our own words. Facts and sources: `RESEARCH.md`.

## Chapter 1: the thought (crew), Vegeta's entrance
- **r1** Someone your age just got the thing you wanted. And you didn't feel happy for them. You felt smaller.
- **r2** Then you felt bad, for feeling that.
- **r3** Dragon Ball Z spent two hundred and fifty episodes on that exact feeling. Its name is Vegeta.
- **r4** Today: why you can't stop comparing yourself. And what to do with it.

## Chapter 2: Vegeta's story (Dragon Ball Z characters)
- **s1** Vegeta is the prince of the Saiyans, a warrior race. Born elite. Goku is a Saiyan too. But he was sent away as a baby, because his power was so low.
- **s2** Vegeta's people wear a device called a scouter. It turns a person into a number. When Goku powers up, Vegeta reads it, and crushes the scouter in his fist.
- **s3** One on one, Vegeta is stronger. It takes four fighters together to bring him down. He still calls it his first great humiliation.
- **s4** Then Goku becomes the legendary Super Saiyan first. The one thing Vegeta believed was his birthright.
- **s5** So he trains alone, at four hundred and fifty times normal gravity, living every day to pass him. And he transforms too. Out of fury at himself, for being second.
- **s6** And Goku still stays ahead.

## Chapter 3: Lesson 1 (Vegeta and Goku, then crew)
- **t1** Lesson one. You only compare with people close to you.
- **t2** Look at who Vegeta picks as a rival. Not the tyrant far above him. Not the fighters far below. The one other Saiyan. Same blood, same trade, never far apart in strength.
- **t3** In nineteen fifty four, the psychologist Leon Festinger proposed that we have a built-in need to know how good we are. And when there's no ruler, we use other people. People close to our own level.
- **t4** A study of college basketball found the same pattern. Rivalry was strongest between teams that were similar, evenly matched, and had met again and again.
- **t5** And a review of sixty years of experiments found that, given the choice, we look up, at someone doing better. And it usually leaves us feeling smaller.
- **t6** Comparing isn't a flaw. It's how your mind takes a measurement.

## Chapter 4: Lesson 2 (Vegeta, runners, Majin Vegeta, crew)
- **u1** Lesson two. A rival can push you, or poison you.
- **u2** The push is real. Vegeta became a Super Saiyan because of Goku. And in a study of amateur runners, people ran a little faster in races where a rival was also running. That's a link, not proof.
- **u3** But the same research group found the other side. In seven seasons of Italian football, matches between same-city rivals drew more yellow cards. And in an experiment, students were about four times as likely to lie to someone from their rival university.
- **u4** That's Vegeta's darkest moment. Years later, at peace, with a family, he lets a wizard's spell in, on purpose. He attacks a stadium crowd, just to force Goku to fight him.
- **u5** Researchers describe two kinds of envy. One says, I want to get up there too. The other says, I want them brought down. In small experiments, only the first kind made people work harder.
- **u6** A rival makes you better while you want to climb. And worse, the moment you'd rather see them fall.

## Chapter 5: Lesson 3 (episode 280, then Nia)
- **v1** Lesson three. Better than you, doesn't mean you're worth less.
- **v2** Episode two hundred and eighty. Goku is fighting the final enemy. Vegeta can only watch. And silently, he runs the comparison one last time.
- **v3** He admits it. Kakarot is better. In the original, he calls him number one.
- **v4** And for the first time, losing the comparison doesn't break him. Because he finally sees it. Goku was never fighting to beat him. He was racing his own limits.
- **v5** There's some evidence for that second ruler. In a study of nearly six hundred kids and teens, feeling better than your past self brought pride, without the urge to be above everyone else. In adults, the evidence is still thin.
- **v6** It stops hurting when, they're better, no longer means, I'm worth less.

## Chapter 6: what to do, care line, close (crew, with Goku and Vegeta in the outro)
- **w1** So here's what I'd suggest. This part is ours, not the studies'.
- **w2** One. Notice who you compare with. It tells you what you want.
- **w3** Two. When it stings, ask one question. What can I learn from them?
- **w4** Three. Keep a second ruler. You, a year ago.
- **w5** And if comparing has you feeling worthless, please talk to someone you trust. There are free support lines in the description.
- **w6** You'll probably never stop comparing. But you can choose what the comparison is for.
