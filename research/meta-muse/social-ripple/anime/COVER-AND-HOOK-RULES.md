# Social Ripple: mission + content rules

## Mission: food for the thought the viewer already has, guiding them correctly
Working assumption: the algorithm shows people what they're thinking about. So every video replies to a thought the viewer already has and takes it somewhere better.

**Arc for every episode and Short:**
1. **Meet the thought:** in their inner voice ("Why do I always quit?").
2. **Show it in the anime:** a character they love has the same thought, so they're not alone with it.
3. **Reframe with real evidence:** what the research actually shows, limits included.
4. **Hand them a better next thought:** one small, doable step.
5. **Let them answer:** a comment prompt that invites their version of the thought.

**Guardrails:**
- Only claims checked against the original papers; never more than a study found.
- No shame, no fear-bait: covers can carry a pain point, but every video ends better than it started.
- Our suggestions are labelled as ours, not findings.
- Heavy topics (loneliness, regret, grief): a gentle "talk to someone you trust" line, plus a support resource in the description.

---


## Depth: what matters most from each lesson
The study is the evidence, the anime is the mirror; the viewer keeps the **truth underneath**.
1. Before writing, state each lesson's core truth in one sentence, and why it matters to the viewer's life. Every scene serves that sentence.
2. Fewer lessons, deeper: three that land beat five that skim. Give each its "so what": how it shows up in their day, what changes if they get it.
3. The core line gets its own moment: a poster card, a beat of silence, the character feeling it.
4. End each lesson on the truth, not the statistic.
5. Close the episode on one sentence that ties the lessons together.

Example (Solo Leveling ep):
- L1 "Still feeling hard doesn't mean it isn't working."
- L2 "Don't wait to feel ready. Decide the moment ahead of time."
- L3 "You can't feel growth day to day, so give yourself a way to see it."
- L4 "The miss isn't the danger. Quitting because of the miss is."
- Whole: "Jinwoo didn't become strong in one day. He became strong by coming back every day, even after the bad ones."

## Cover + first-frame rules (from the ice-water Short's results)

Day-1 data, "They Fooled Themselves With Ice Water" Short: 242 views, 0.8 h watch time ≈ 12 s average on 43 s (~25–30% viewed).
Read: the cover pulled people in; the slow-drawing first frame lost them.

## Why the cover worked (user's observations)
1. **Relatable visually:** an everyday scene anyone has lived (cold water, a bucket, a stopwatch), readable in under a second.
2. **Body sensation / pain point:** you almost feel the cold (shiver lines), an embodied "ouch" that stops the thumb.
3. **Mystery:** "They fooled *themselves*", a curiosity gap.
4. **Specific number:** "only 9 of 38 admitted it".

## Formula
**Relatable situation + body sensation + mystery + specific number**, with the anime character placed *inside* an everyday moment (not just posing).

## Shorts rules
- First frame = the cover image, fully drawn, big text on screen. No blank paper, no slow draw-on at the start.
- Hook line under 2 s, asking the question the Short answers.
- 20–30 s, one idea; the last line loops into the first.
- An anime character in every Short.

## Check after each upload
Studio → Analytics → Content → Shorts: "Viewed vs. swiped away" + retention graph; Reach → Traffic source (Shorts feed = first frame matters; search/channel/browse = cover matters).

## Core principle: mirror what viewers are already thinking (applies to EVERYTHING)
The algorithm matches content to people through the footprints of their thoughts: searches, watches, rewatches, pauses, comments. Content that voices what someone is already thinking gets shown to them.
- **Topic:** start from a question people already ask themselves ("why do I keep quitting?"), then find the anime moment that answers it. Sources: YouTube search autocomplete ("why do I…", "how to stop…"), comments under anime clips, our own comments.
- **Title:** in the viewer's inner voice ("Why You Quit After 2 Weeks (Solo Leveling Explains)"), with the anime name for search.
- **Cover:** the moment they've personally lived, inside the anime world.
- **First line:** say their thought back to them before any facts ("You start strong. Then by week three, you're done.").
- **Captions:** highlight the words they'd use in their own head.
- **Comment prompt:** ask for their version of the thought ("What's the habit you keep restarting?").
- **Shorts → episode:** each Short voices one thought; the long video is the full answer.
