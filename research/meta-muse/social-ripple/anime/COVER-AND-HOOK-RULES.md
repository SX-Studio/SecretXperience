# Cover + first-frame rules (from the ice-water Short's results)

Day-1 data, "They Fooled Themselves With Ice Water" Short: 242 views, 0.8 h watch time ≈ 12 s average on 43 s (~25–30% viewed).
Read: the cover pulled people in; the slow-drawing first frame lost them.

## Why the cover worked (user's observations)
1. **Relatable visually:** an everyday scene anyone has lived (cold water, a bucket, a stopwatch), readable in under a second.
2. **Body sensation / pain point:** you almost feel the cold (shiver lines), an embodied "ouch" that stops the thumb.
3. **Mystery:** "They fooled *themselves*", a curiosity gap.
4. **Specific number:** "only 9 of 38 admitted it".

## Formula
**Relatable situation + body sensation + mystery + specific number**, with the anime character placed *inside* an everyday moment (not just posing).

## Shorts rules
- First frame = the cover image, fully drawn, big text on screen. No blank paper, no slow draw-on at the start.
- Hook line under 2 s, asking the question the Short answers.
- 20–30 s, one idea; the last line loops into the first.
- An anime character in every Short.

## Check after each upload
Studio → Analytics → Content → Shorts: "Viewed vs. swiped away" + retention graph; Reach → Traffic source (Shorts feed = first frame matters; search/channel/browse = cover matters).
