## Why it holds people

| Technique | What the video does |
|---|---|
| **Opens on the consequence** | In the first 5 s, Dad calls 911 about "a kid in my house". The premise follows by 15 s. Mystery comes first, then the setup. |
| **A rule set stated early** | Three numbered rules give viewers a game to track. A rule's "fine print" pays off at the very end. |
| **Numbered problems** | "Problem 1… Problem 4". Each complication changes the rules, so the steps never feel repetitive. |
| **One step every ~19 s** | 30 nights in about 570 s. Act 1 is fast and fun. Act 2 slows to explore flaws. Act 3 has a rapid montage, then a slow countdown climax. |
| **A gut-punch at the midpoint** | Night 20: Dad forgets his own son. This is the **most-liked comment** by far. The emotional, family-relationship beat beats every superpower. |
| **Real science at the low point** | A real neuroscience case (patient H.M.) grounds the impossible premise. |
| **"You" narration** | It is deadpan, with short sentences and fragments. Viewers project themselves into it: most comments are "I'd probably…". |
| **A persistent HUD** | A NIGHT counter, a countdown clock, and a second metric ("people who know you"). Some text or graphic changes every 3–8 s. |
| **Sound** | A drone that builds through countdowns, piano for emotion, dense UI sound effects, and **total silence on the key realisation**. |
| **Ending** | About 45 s after the climax it closes on a reflective question. No "subscribe" ask. |

## For Social Ripple

**Adopt:**
1. **Open on the emotional consequence**, then rewind to day 1. This is a stronger version of our Episode 4 "Day 30: ???" flash.
2. **State 2–3 simple rules on screen early**, and pay off one rule's fine print at the end.
3. **Numbered complications** ("Problem 2"), each changing the situation.
4. **A family or close-relationship gut-punch at the midpoint**, the moment people comment on.
5. **One fascinating real-science anchor at the low point.**
6. **"You" framing plus a closing question**, which invites "I would…" comments.
7. **A second HUD metric** next to the counter (our Episode 4 hello tally and talk meter already do this).
8. **Silence on the realisation beat.**
9. **A "science mode" look** (blueprint or paper) for research beats.
10. **Comparison stacks** for numbers.
11. **One action colour** for important on-screen elements.

**Don't copy:**
- 911 calls or horror and panic framing.
- 45–50 cuts a minute. That clashes with our calm, therapeutic tone, and MrBeast's former editor says hyper-cutting is fading.
- The lineless flat style. The user loves our inked, toon-shaded 3D world.

**Length:** this video is 10 minutes at ~19 s a step. Our 3–6 minute format fits **10–14 steps** at the same pace.

## Format ideas in this style (calm, research-based)

- **"What If You Could Hear What People Really Thought of You for 7 Days?"** Uses the spotlight effect and the liking gap. Midpoint: they think about you far less, and far more kindly, than you feared.
- **"What If Every Kind Thing You Did Came Back to You the Next Day?"** The ripple, made literal.
- **"What If You Felt Everyone's Mood for 30 Days?"** Emotional contagion and empathy fatigue. Midpoint: feeling a parent's hidden sadness.
