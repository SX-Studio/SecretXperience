### Concrete Techniques for a 3D-Toon Stick-Figure Channel

1.  **The "Information Halo":** Surround your character with floating icons (0:10). In 3D, these can be billboards that always face the camera, representing the character's status or current "perks."
2.  **Luminance as Narrative:** Use a singular "Action Color" (like the teal #2BFFC5) for all interactive or important elements, keeping the rest of the 3D environment in muted, dark tones.
3.  **Modular Set Dressing:** Keep 3D environments sparse. Use 2D-style "posters" or flat-shaded furniture to maintain the "What If" infographic aesthetic while using 3D for depth.
4.  **Blueprint Mode Shaders:** For technical explanations, switch your 3D models to a "Wireframe" or "Toon Outline" shader with a black background (similar to 1:39) to signify a shift from "story" to "fact."
5.  **Dynamic UI Counters:** Keep a persistent "Counter" or "Clock" in the corner (0:14). In 3D, you can animate this in world-space as a floating holographic projection.
6.  **Comparison Stacks:** To explain size or quantity, use the "Spider Stack" technique (0:53). Line up 3D stick figures to show scale (e.g., "How many stick figures tall is the Eiffel Tower?").
7.  **Expressive Eye Scaling:** Don't rig complex faces. Simply scale the "eye" meshes/textures larger or smaller to convey surprise, anger, or fatigue.
8.  **Vignette Focus:** Use a dark vignette or "spotlight" lighting to darken the corners of the 3D scene, forcing the viewer to look at the stick figure in the center.

### 3 Things to Avoid
1.  **High-Detail Textures:** Avoid realistic wood grains or fabrics. They clash with the stick-figure aesthetic. Use flat colors or very simple procedural gradients.
2.  **Slow Panning Shots:** Infographic-style videos thrive on "snappy" movement. Avoid long, cinematic 3D drone shots; they kill the educational momentum.
3.  **Complex Character Outlines:** In 3D, thick "ink" outlines can get messy during fast movement. Stick to the lineless "flat" look used in this video for better clarity.
