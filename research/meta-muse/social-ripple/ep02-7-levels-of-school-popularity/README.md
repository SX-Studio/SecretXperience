## Research (verified 2026-11-01)

- **Two kinds of popularity.** Mitch Prinstein (UNC) separates status from likeability, and links chasing status to later anxiety, depression, substance use and relationship problems, and likeability to better relationships and careers. Sources: *Popular* (2017) and UNC's "Likes vs. likeability". The film says "links", not "causes".
- **A pecking order in three days.** Savin-Williams (1977), *Animal Behaviour*: a dominance hierarchy among 13-year-old boys at summer camp was evident after 3 days and stayed stable for 5 weeks.
- **False news spreads farther and faster.** Vosoughi, Roy & Aral (2018), *Science* 359:1146: about 126,000 stories on Twitter, 2006–2017. The "novelty" explanation comes from the paper.
- **The 56-school experiment.** Paluck, Shepherd & Aronow (2016), *PNAS* 113:566: 56 New Jersey middle schools and 24,191 students. Disciplinary reports of conflict fell 30% over one year compared with control schools, and the effect was strongest when the seed group included more "social referents".
- **The "cool kids" follow-up.** Allen et al. (2014), *Child Development*: 184 teens followed from 13 into their early 20s. By 22, peers rated those who had acted "cool" early as less socially competent.
