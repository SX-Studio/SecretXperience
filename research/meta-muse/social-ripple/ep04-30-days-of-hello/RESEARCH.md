# Ep4 research: facts used on screen (verified 2026-11-02)

Each claim was checked against the primary paper's abstract on PubMed or Consensus. The Surgeon General PDF was blocked (403), so claim 1 was checked against coverage of the advisory and the papers it cites.

| Script line | What we say | Source | Notes / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| n04 | About half of US adults say they experience loneliness | US Surgeon General (2023), *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation*, HHS | Broad survey scales. Stricter measures are lower (Gallup 2023: 17% lonely "a lot of yesterday"). |
| n04 | Loneliness works like hunger: a signal that you need people | Cacioppo & Hawkley (2009), *Trends Cogn Sci* 13(10):447–454 (theory) | Metaphor, framed as such. |
| n08–n09 | A lonely brain can get extra alert to rejection, which looks cold but is protective | Cacioppo & Hawkley (2009), doi:10.1016/j.tics.2009.06.005 | A theoretical model from a review, introduced in the script as "researchers think". |
| n14 | Commuters asked to talk to a stranger enjoyed the ride more; people predicted the opposite | Epley & Schroeder (2014), *J Exp Psychol Gen* 143(5):1980–1999, doi:10.1037/a0037323 | Chicago trains and buses; self-reported. |
| n15 | A short warm chat with a barista lifted mood more than rushing | Sandstrom & Dunn (2014), *Soc Psychol Personal Sci* 5(4):437–442, doi:10.1177/1948550613502990 | N=60; positive affect. |
| n21 | The liking gap: after talking, we underestimate how much the other person liked us | Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark (2018), *Psychol Sci* 29(11):1742–1756, doi:10.1177/0956797618783714 | Replicated across settings. |
| n25 | People appreciate being reached out to more than we expect | Liu, Rim, Min & Min (2023), *J Pers Soc Psychol* 124(4):754–771, doi:10.1037/pspi0000402 | Preregistered experiments. |
| n27 | Roughly 50 hours together to become casual friends | Hall (2019), *J Soc Pers Relat* 36(4):1278–1296, doi:10.1177/0265407518761225 | Estimates from recalled hours; from the KU press release; "roughly". |

**Framing:** the 30-day arc is a **story**, labelled on screen as "a story built on real research". No study ran a 30-day hello intervention. The closest is Sandstrom, Boothby & Cooney (2022), *J Exp Soc Psychol* 102:104356: a week of talking to strangers reduced fear of rejection.

## Not claimed (deliberately)

- The "15 cigarettes a day" comparison (contested).
- "50% longer life" (it is 50% higher *odds of survival*, from observational data: Holt-Lunstad 2010).
- "Loneliness is contagious" (the Cacioppo, Fowler & Christakis 2009 network clustering is observational and disputed).
- "Talking to strangers cures loneliness."
