# Ep5 research: facts used on screen (verified 2026-11-04)

The spotlight-effect numbers were checked in the full paper. The other papers were checked through their abstracts on PubMed or Consensus, so **no numbers from them go on screen**.

| Line | What we say | Source | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| n08 | Students in an embarrassing T-shirt guessed about half the room would remember it; about a quarter did | Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), *JPSP* 78(2):211–222, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211, Study 1: 46% estimated vs 23% actual | 15 wearers; the measure is observers who could identify what was on the shirt |
| n12 | We assume our nerves show; people see much less than we think (illusion of transparency) | Gilovich, Savitsky & Medvec (1998), *JPSP* 75(2):332–346, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.75.2.332 | No numbers on screen |
| n14 | We expect slip-ups to be judged more harshly than they are; observers picture themselves in our shoes | Savitsky, Epley & Gilovich (2001), *JPSP* 81(1):44–56, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.81.1.44; Epley, Savitsky & Gilovich (2002), *JPSP* 83(2):300–312 (empathy neglect) | "Less harshly than we think", not "no one judges" |
| n22 | We judge our own vulnerable moments more harshly than the same moments in others (the beautiful mess effect) | Bruk, Scholl & Bless (2018), *JPSP* 115(2):192–205, doi:10.1037/pspa0000120 | Mostly imagined scenarios, comparing how people rate themselves vs others |
| n27 | Being kind to yourself after a mistake raised motivation to improve | Breines & Chen (2012), *PSPB* 38(9):1133–1143, doi:10.1177/0146167212445599 | Short-term lab experiments, compared against a self-esteem boost |

**Background (not quoted on screen):** Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky (2008), "Rethinking rumination". Rumination is linked to a higher risk of depression and anxiety; this is not a diagnosis.

## Not claimed

- "Nobody notices or judges you."
- "Everyone overestimates by 2×."
- The name on the T-shirt.
- That learning about the illusion "cures" nerves.
- That overthinking causes disorders.
- That self-compassion is proven better long-term.
