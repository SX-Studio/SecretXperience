## S02 who Mo is + rules
n03: This is Mo. Twenty-two years of sandwiches. Same shop. Same apron. A life he'd call... small.
n04: Then, one Tuesday night, while he was locking up, Mo started to glow.
n05: Golden threads. Hundreds of them. And they came with rules.
n06: One: every thread is a life you've touched. Two: the brighter the thread, the more it mattered to them. Three: pull a thread, and that moment never happened. Rule three should have come with a warning.

## S03 the brightest thread → Theo (study 1: small kindness underestimated)
n07: The brightest thread led somewhere Mo didn't expect.
n08: Theo. Last spring, his very first week on the island. Lonely. Hungry. Counting coins.
mo_01 (Mo): On the house. Welcome to the island.
n09: Mo forgot about it by lunchtime. Theo never did.
n10: Turns out, that's normal. In one study, people handed out free hot chocolate at a winter skating rink. The givers guessed it would mean a little. The people who got it said it meant a lot more.
n11: We tend to underestimate how much our small kindnesses matter.

## S04 the thread keeps going (study 2: cooperation cascades, lab game)
n12: And the thread didn't stop at Theo.
n13: That sandwich was the first kind thing anyone did for Theo here. It wasn't the last. These days, Theo says "Morning!" to everyone. When Bea the baker dropped a whole tower of trays, he was the first to help. And Bea started baking an extra batch for her neighbour, who lives alone.
n14: One sandwich. Three lives.
n15: In a lab game with real money, when one player gave a little more, people two and even three steps away started giving more too. People who had never even met them.

## S05 midpoint: the thickest thread → Kai; Mo pulls it
n16: Then Mo found the thickest thread of all. It ran all the way down to the harbour.
n17: Eleven years ago. A storm. A soaked stranger with a suitcase, waiting for the last boat off the island.
mo_02 (Mo): We're closed, but... come in. The soup's still hot.
n18: That stranger was Kai. He missed the boat. He stayed. And he built half the houses on this island.
n19: Mo wondered what the island would look like without that one night. Just for a second. So he pulled.
n20: The houses. The porches. The families inside them. Gone.
mo_03 (Mo): No, no, no...
n21: Mo pushed the thread back as fast as he could.

## S06 study 3: mental subtraction
n22: There's an experiment about this exact feeling. People wrote about a good thing in their life, but imagined how it might never have happened.
n23: It lifted their mood more than writing about how it did happen. Most people expected the opposite.
n24: Sometimes you only see what something means when you picture it gone.

## S07 payoff (dawn; Theo's note) + study 4 + takeaway
n25: By sunrise, the threads had faded. Mo opened the shop like any other day.
theo_01 (Theo): I never said thank you. So... I wrote it down.
n26: The note was three lines long. Mo read it four times.
n27: And one last thread lit up. Pointing the other way. Toward him.
n28 (Nia on screen): You'll never see your threads. But they're there. So try this: think of one person who mattered to you, and tell them. In studies of thank-you letters, writers underestimated how good it made the other person feel, and overestimated how awkward it would be.

## S08 close
n29: Mo thought his life was small. Turns out, nobody's is.
