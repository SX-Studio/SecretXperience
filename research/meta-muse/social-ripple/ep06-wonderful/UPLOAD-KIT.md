## Description
Mo has run the same sandwich shop for 22 years. He thinks he's a nobody, just a guy who makes tuna melts.

Then one Tuesday night, golden threads burst out of him and stretch to everyone his small kindnesses ever reached. There are three rules. The third one should have come with a warning.

This is an animated story, but every study in it is real.

If your life feels small right now, it probably reaches further than you can see. And if it's heavy, talking to someone you trust, or to a mental-health professional, can help.

Research in this episode:
• Kumar & Epley (2023). A little good goes an unexpectedly long way: Underestimating the positive impact of kindness on recipients. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General.
• Fowler & Christakis (2010). Cooperative behavior cascades in human social networks. PNAS. (A lab game with real money.)
• Koo, Algoe, Wilson & Gilbert (2008). It's a wonderful life: Mentally subtracting positive events improves people's affective states. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.
• Kumar & Epley (2018). Undervaluing gratitude: Expressers misunderstand the consequences of showing appreciation. Psychological Science.

Today's tiny experiment: tell one person what they did that mattered to you.
