## Description
One morning, you wake up and can see everyone's inner weather. Sun over the people who are doing fine. Clouds, rain and storms over the ones who aren't. Their faces never change.

There are three rules. The third one is the one that matters.

This is an animated story, but every study in it is real.

If you're the one under the storm right now, you don't have to carry it alone. Ask someone for help: they'll probably say yes. And if it feels heavy, talking to a mental-health professional can help.

Research in this episode:
• Jordan, Monin, Dweck, Lovett, John & Gross (2011). Misery has more company than people think: Underestimating the prevalence of others' negative emotions. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. (Students underestimated classmates' hard times. Underestimating was linked to feeling lonelier, which is a correlation, not proof of cause. Students reported hiding about 40% of their bad moments vs 13% of their good ones.)
• Flynn & Lake (2008). If you need help, just ask: Underestimating compliance with direct requests for help. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. (People guessed they'd need to ask about 20 strangers to get 5 to do a small favour; it took about 10.)

"Ask twice" is our gentle suggestion, not a study finding.

Today's tiny experiment: ask someone how they're doing. If they say "fine," ask one more time.
