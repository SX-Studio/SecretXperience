# Ep8: "What If You Could See Who Is Lying to You?" (scene cards and script)

**The one mystery that carries the whole episode** (from the ConChaser study): *why has the friendliest person on the island told 37 lies by breakfast?*
- **Plants and payoffs:** YOU's own three small lies (speech bubbles, never counted on screen) pay off at the mirror. Mo's "best coffee" pays off in the mirror ending. The numbers climbing around YOU pay off at the party.
- **Devices:** a glowing red counter over every head (pops and ticks), time-of-day headers, the rules card, a coin-flip insert, and the black title card.
- **Voices:** YOU never speaks aloud (speech bubbles only). Nia narrates with the Kokoro af_heart voice. Characters are acted with Gemini TTS.

## Narration (Nia, Kokoro af_heart)
- n01 Today, you can see a number over everyone's head. It counts every lie they've told today.
- n02 Mo's coffee is fine. It is not the best.
- n03 Most of the numbers are tiny. And then there's Pip.
- n04 Thirty-seven. It's seven thirty-one in the morning.
- n05 By midnight, you'll know why. And you won't like your own number.
- n06 Rule one. It counts every lie, big or small.
- n07 Rule two. It resets at midnight.
- n08 Rule three. Nobody else can see it.
- n09 You check the whole island. Zero. Zero. Zero.
- n10 In one survey of a thousand American adults, about six in ten said they hadn't told a single lie that day.
- n11 So who is Pip lying to?
- n12 In that same survey, half of all the lies came from about five people in every hundred.
- n13 A few people tell most of the lies. And today, one of them is Pip.
- n14 By the afternoon, everyone you love is lying to you. And without this power, you'd never know.
- n15 People catch a lie only about fifty-four percent of the time. Barely better than flipping a coin.
- n16 Every lie today was hiding the same thing.
- n17 In one diary study, about one in four everyday lies was told for someone else's sake.
- n18 Even honest people have bad lie days.
- n19 And there, in the window, is your own number. Twelve.
- n20 Best ever. On my way. I'm not mad.
- n21 People in that study said they didn't think their lies were serious, and barely worried about getting caught.
- n22 Researchers who reviewed decades of lie studies suggest we judge other people's lies more harshly than our own.
- n23 You'll never see anyone's number. But in a study that followed people's lies for three months, almost nine in ten lies were little white lies, and people said lies were only about seven percent of everything they said. So believing people is usually a safe bet. And the next time you're about to tell a little one, just notice it.
- n24 Everyone tells a few. Most people, most of the time, are telling you the truth.
