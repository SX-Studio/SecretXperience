## Description
```
Today, you can see a number over everyone's head. It counts every lie they've told today. Most people are at zero. And then there's Pip: 37 lies by 7:31 in the morning.

By midnight, you'll know why. And you won't like your own number.

This is an animated story, but every study in it is real.

Research in this episode:
• Serota, Levine & Boster (2010). The prevalence of lying in America: Three studies of self-reported lies. Human Communication Research. (1,000 US adults: about 6 in 10 reported no lies that day; half of all lies came from about 5% of people.)
• Serota, Levine & Docan-Morgan (2021). Unpacking variation in lie prevalence: Prolific liars, bad lie days, or both? Communication Monographs. (116,366 lies over 91 days: almost 90% were little white lies; lies were about 7% of communication.)
• DePaulo, Kashy, Kirkendol, Wyer & Epstein (1996). Lying in everyday life. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. (About 1 in 4 everyday lies was told for someone else's sake.)
• Bond & DePaulo (2006). Accuracy of deception judgments. Personality and Social Psychology Review. (People catch lies about 54% of the time.)

"Just notice your own little ones" is our gentle suggestion, not a study finding.

Today's tiny experiment: count your own little lies for one day. No judging, just noticing.
```
