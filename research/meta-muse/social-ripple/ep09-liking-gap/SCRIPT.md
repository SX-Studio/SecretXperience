## Narration (Nia)
- n01 After every conversation, a number appears over the other person's head. How much they actually liked talking to you. Out of ten.
- n02 And in your head, there's another number. Your guess.
- n03 Five minutes with Juno. You're already sure it went badly.
- n04 The "you too." The weird laugh. The goodbye.
- n05 You give yourself a two.
- n06 She gave you a nine.
- n07 That can't be right. So you test it.
- n08 Mo. You guess three.
- n09 Finn. You guess four.
- n10 Bea. You guess two.
- n11 Every single time, you guess low.
- n12 After a conversation, people tend to underestimate how much the other person liked them and enjoyed their company. Researchers call it the liking gap.
- n13 In those studies, people were too wrapped up in worrying about what they'd said to notice the signs that the other person was enjoying it.
- n14 And then you see Juno.
- n15 She guessed you gave her a three. You gave her a nine.
- n16 Two people, both sure the other one wasn't interested. About to never talk again.
- n17 In one study of college roommates, that gap lasted for several months.
- n18 And remember how your chat ended? You said, I'll let you go.
- n19 In one study of nearly a thousand conversations, only about two percent ended when both people wanted them to.
- n20 She wasn't done talking. Neither were you.
- n21 Ten out of ten.
- n22 You'll never see the number. But after your next conversation, when your brain replays every awkward second, remember: they probably liked you more than you think. And if a conversation was good, you're allowed to keep it going.
- n26 Weeks of awkward little waves. And never another real conversation.
- n28 Kai. You guess one.
- n29 So you go back.
- n30 The next day, you two talk for two hours.
- n23 Turns out, the only one giving you a two was you.
