## Description
```
After every conversation, a number appears over the other person's head: how much they actually liked talking to you. And in your head, there's another number. Your guess.

Five minutes with Juno, and you're sure it went badly. You give yourself a two. She gave you a nine.

This is an animated story, but every study in it is real.

Research in this episode:
• Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark (2018). The liking gap in conversations: Do people like us more than we think? Psychological Science. (After conversations, people underestimated how much their partner liked them and enjoyed their company. In a study of college roommates, the gap lasted for several months.)
• Mastroianni, Gilbert, Cooney & Wilson (2021). Do conversations end when people want them to? PNAS. (In 932 conversations, only about 2% ended when both people wanted them to.)

Today's tiny experiment: after your next conversation, when your brain starts replaying the awkward bits, give yourself the number they'd probably give you instead.
```
