# Short 02: The Christmas Truce (51.8 s, 1080×1920)

This one is on-purpose for the channel: **a ripple between people.** One trench sings, the next copies, and about 100,000 enemies stop shooting.

**Facts (verified on Wikipedia, "Christmas truce", 2026-11-01):**
- 24–26 Dec 1914 on the Western Front.
- Germans put candles and small Christmas trees on their trenches and sang carols. The British sang back.
- Men met unarmed in no-man's-land, swapped food, tobacco, buttons and hats, and buried their dead together.
- About 100,000 British and German troops took part.
- Football is mostly informal kick-abouts; one account says the ball was a bully-beef tin.
- A German order of 29 Dec 1914 banned fraternising. In Dec 1915, Allied commanders ordered raids and artillery barrages to prevent a repeat.

The famous scored match (from Graves's retelling) and the 1915 Thessaloniki football photo are **not** used.

**Formula:**
1. Hook: "one Christmas song made a hundred thousand enemies stop shooting".
2. Setting.
3. Escalation: candles, trees, singing, the reply, one man climbs out.
4. Beat: "Nobody shoots", held in silence.
5. "You see": everyone copies the man next to them. A map of the front turns gold trench by trench, with a counter to about 100,000.
6. Payoff: the swaps, the shared burial, the tin of beef.
7. Twist: the generals' 1915 order.
8. Lesson: "Peace spreads the same way war does. Somebody goes first."

**Sound:** a synthesized music-box *Silent Night* (Gruber, 1818, public domain) from the German side, answered in a lower voice from the British side and returning for the ending, plus a warm cue from ACE-Step.
