## 1. "Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work" (Ricks Clips, Short, 1:05)
**Why it works:**
- **A contradiction in the first second.** "He sawed gaps into a railroad every 39 feet." Why would anyone sabotage their own track?
- **It hides the lesson inside a misdirection.** The hero looks wrong, everyone mocks him (the "Clickety-Clack Line") and he loses the race. Then summer comes, the gaps close, and the "perfect" rail kicks sideways like a snake. The science (thermal expansion) arrives as the twist, never as a lecture.
- **Concrete units and images instead of abstractions.** "39 feet", "finger-wide", "a coin in May, a sheet of paper by July", "rail touching rail".
- **Emotion made physical.** The boss's face turns purple and steam blows out of his hat.
- **A tool on screen makes time and change visible.** A caliper measures the gap shrinking month by month.
- **The narrator voices characters' lines inside the narration.** "You're wrecking my railroad!" The tone shifts per beat: deadpan, then mysterious, then ominous, then fast.
- **Every action has a sound.** Saw, steam hiss, clickety-clack, metal groan, boom, then a sudden stop.
- **Word-by-word kinetic captions.** White text with yellow keywords, 1–4 words at a time, synced to the voice.
- **Fast cutting.** About 15 shots in 65 s, with whip pans, push-ins and macro close-ups for the key evidence.

## 2. "What If Modulo Yuji Was In Black Clover?" (Red Pill POV, 19 min)
**Why it works as a story, not a lecture:**
- **The premise is a variant plus a wound.** "Not the boy who believes he can save everyone… the Yuji who lost his teacher, lost his friends."
- **Hard rules on cards early, then tested immediately by concrete trials.** No mana, so he can't fly a broom. Therefore he builds a blood platform. Every scene is *but/therefore*.
- **Invisible things are drawn as objects.** Glowing mana orbs in children's chests, an aura swirling against empty air, slice lines, a scar closing under glowing hands.
- **The palette carries the world state.** Grey and crimson for grief, green and gold for the new home, black and neon for the big fights.
- **A re-hook about every 30 s.** A new rival or obstacle arrives just as the last one resolves. Open loops: "which squad?", "can he still use his domain?"
- **The ending calls back the opening.** He begins alone at a grave and ends sitting with a friend: "Home isn't a place. Home is the people who are waiting for you."
- **Cuts are fast** (estimated 1.4–1.8 s average), but the emotional beats get longer holds and soft piano.

## What we change from Ep8 on (ranked by impact)
1. **Hide the lesson until the twist.** Build the episode as misdirection, then reveal. Let the viewer believe the wrong thing for a while (like the railroad), so the research lands as a plot twist rather than a chart.
2. **Show research inside the world, never as a slide.** Ep7's viewer review flagged our charts as "school presentation", and we already moved one into a classroom of people. Do the same every time: calipers, counters on objects, people, weather.
3. **Use word-by-word captions with highlighted keywords** in the yellow and white brand style. This would be a new layer in the engine: we have word timings for every line already. Test it on the next episode or Short.
4. **Make an emotion physical when it peaks.** Steam, colour floods, the room tilting, rain falling onto the head (added in Ep7). Pair each one with a strong sound.
5. **Write every scene as but/therefore.** Rules are tested within seconds of being stated. Give the power a cost or limit that creates a problem to solve.
6. **Put a re-hook or open loop every ~30 s.** Pose a question before you answer it.
7. **Call back the opening at the end.** The first image comes back transformed. Ep7 did this with the square, so make it deliberate every time.
8. **Use concrete numbers and images in the script.** "20 strangers vs 10" works; "a lot of people" doesn't.
9. **Shift the palette per world state.** Grade and lighting follow the arc; Ep7 has the system.
10. **Have the narrator voice character lines** at short punchlines, to keep the rhythm. We now also have acted Gemini voices for longer lines.
