## The slate
Each episode is built on one **real, solid study** (to be verified again word-for-word before scripting, like every episode). None of them repeats a topic ConChaser has already made, or one we've already made.

### Ep 8: What If You Could See Who Is Lying to You?
- **Story:** For one day, a red number floats over everyone who lies. Your sweetest coworker sits at 0, but the friend who always says "love the haircut!" is at 37. The twist: most people tell almost no lies, and a few tell most of them.
- **Research:** DePaulo et al. 1996, diary study (about 1–2 small lies a day); Serota, Levine & Boster 2010 (about half of all lies come from roughly 5% of people); Bond & DePaulo 2006 (people spot lies about 54% of the time).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See Who Is Lying to You?". A bright modern office kitchen. Left foreground: YOU, holding a coffee mug, frozen mid-sip with wide shocked eyes and a sweat drop, side-eyeing to the right. Right, mid-ground: a friendly-looking young woman with a huge sweet smile, waving at him. Above her head a glowing RED neon number "37" with a small red arrow pointing down at her. Text in the image: only "37".
```

### Ep 9: What If You Could See What People Really Think of You?
- **Story:** After every conversation you see two scores: how much you think they liked you, and how much they actually did. Yours are always lower. You've been underestimating everyone's opinion of you.
- **Research:** Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark 2018, "The Liking Gap" (after talking, people underestimate how much their partner liked them).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See What People Really Think of You?". A cosy café at golden hour, two people just finished talking at a small table. Left: YOU, slumped and cringing, hand on forehead, above his head a glowing RED neon "2/10" with an arrow. Right: a friendly young man walking away, smiling warmly back at YOU, above his head a glowing GREEN neon "9/10" with an arrow. Text in the image: only "2/10" and "9/10".
```

### Ep 10: What If You Could See Which of Your Worries Will Actually Happen?
- **Story:** Every worry floats above your head as a little storm, tagged "WILL HAPPEN" or "WON'T". Night after night, almost all of them say "WON'T".
- **Research:** LaFreniere & Newman 2020, *Behavior Therapy* (in people with anxiety, about 91% of worry predictions did not come true). It was a small study; we'll say so on screen.
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See Which of Your Worries Will Actually Happen?". Night, a dark bedroom lit by a phone screen. YOU sitting up in bed, eyes huge with anxiety, clutching the blanket. Above him a giant swirling dark thought-cloud full of tiny disasters (a car crash, an angry boss, a broken phone, a crying face). Over the cloud a glowing GREEN neon label "91% NEVER HAPPEN" with an arrow pointing at the cloud. Text in the image: only "91% NEVER HAPPEN".
```

### Ep 11: What If You Could See Who Would Help You in an Emergency?
- **Story:** You trip in a busy street and a word appears above every stranger's head. You expect everyone to walk past (the famous "bystander effect"), but real CCTV footage tells a different story.
- **Research:** Philpot et al. 2020, *American Psychologist* (219 real public conflicts filmed on CCTV in three countries: someone stepped in about 9 times out of 10). Darley & Latané 1968 gives the classic background.
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See Who Would Help You in an Emergency?". A busy city sidewalk, golden hour. YOU has just fallen on the pavement, looking up, shocked and embarrassed, groceries spilled. Behind him a blurred crowd of strangers; above EVERY stranger's head a small glowing GREEN neon check mark. Big glowing GREEN neon text above the crowd: "9 OUT OF 10". Text in the image: only "9 OUT OF 10".
```

### Ep 12: What If You Could See Who Is Pretending to Understand?
- **Story:** In class, every nodding head gets a glowing "???" above it. Nobody asks because everyone thinks they're the only one who's lost. You raise your hand.
- **Research:** Miller & McFarland 1987 (pluralistic ignorance: why nobody asks questions in class); Rozenblit & Keil 2002 (the illusion of explanatory depth).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See Who Is Pretending to Understand?". A sunlit classroom, a teacher's blackboard covered in complicated equations (blurred). Foreground left: YOU, nervously raising one hand, sweat drop, side-eyeing the class. Behind him, several students confidently nodding, and above EVERY one of their heads a glowing BLUE neon "???". Text in the image: only "???".
```

### Ep 13: What If You Could See How Much Someone Wants to Hear From You?
- **Story:** Above every old friend in your contacts you see one number: how happy they'd be if you texted right now. It's always higher than you think.
- **Research:** Liu, Rim, Min & Min 2023, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, "The Surprise of Reaching Out" (people underestimate how much others appreciate hearing from them).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See How Much Someone Wants to Hear From You?". Split by distance: left foreground, YOU at night holding his phone, thumb hovering over "Send", biting his lip, unsure. Right background, in a warm window far away, an old friend looking at her own phone, hopeful. Above the friend a glowing GREEN neon "100%" with a little heart and an arrow. Text in the image: only "100%".
```

### Ep 14: What If You Could See What Actually Makes Everyone Happy?
- **Story:** Above each person floats the one thing that predicts their long-term happiness. It's rarely their money, job or looks. It's their relationships.
- **Research:** The Harvard Study of Adult Development (running since 1938; Waldinger & Schulz, *The Good Life*, 2023).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See What Actually Makes Everyone Happy?". Two scenes side by side in one image. Left: a rich man alone in a huge cold marble mansion, gold watch, staring sadly into a glass; above him a glowing RED neon "3/10". Right: YOU laughing with two friends around a cheap pizza in a small warm kitchen; above YOU a glowing GREEN neon "9/10". Text in the image: only "3/10" and "9/10".
```

### Ep 15: What If You Could See How Much Sleep Everyone Got?
- **Story:** Everyone has a glowing hours counter. The grumpy, withdrawn people aren't mean; they're running on 4 hours, and sleep loss changes how social we feel.
- **Research:** Ben Simon & Walker 2018, *Nature Communications* (one night of sleep loss made people more socially withdrawn and lonelier, and others rated them as lonelier too).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See How Much Sleep Everyone Got?". Morning on a train platform. Left foreground: YOU, wide awake and cheerful, holding a coffee, above him a glowing GREEN neon "8H". Right: a coworker with huge dark eye bags and messy hair, scowling, almost falling asleep standing, above him a glowing RED neon "3H". Text in the image: only "8H" and "3H".
```

### Ep 16: What If You Could See Who Is Secretly Copying You?
- **Story:** A glowing line appears every time someone mirrors your posture, words or laugh, and it turns out that's how people bond without knowing it. Then you notice who never mirrors you.
- **Research:** Chartrand & Bargh 1999, the "chameleon effect" (people unconsciously mimic each other, and being mimicked increases liking).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See Who Is Secretly Copying You?". A park bench at golden hour. Left: YOU, arms crossed, one leg crossed, eyebrow raised in suspicion. Right: a young woman sitting in EXACTLY the same pose, mirror-image, same raised eyebrow. Glowing GREEN neon dotted lines connect their matching arms and legs. Above her head a glowing GREEN neon "COPYING YOU" with an arrow. Text in the image: only "COPYING YOU".
```

### Ep 17: What If You Could See Who Would Return Your Lost Wallet?
- **Story:** You drop a wallet stuffed with cash, and over each stranger's head you see "RETURNS IT" or "KEEPS IT". The twist from the real experiment: more money in the wallet made people more honest, not less.
- **Research:** Cohn, Maréchal, Tannenbaum & Zünd 2019, *Science* (about 17,000 "lost" wallets in 40 countries; wallets with money were returned more often than empty ones).
- **PROMPT:**
```
Thumbnail for "What If You Could See Who Would Return Your Lost Wallet?". A busy street, golden hour. Foreground: a fat leather wallet on the pavement, stuffed with cash, glowing slightly. Left: YOU, patting his empty pockets, mouth open in panic. Right: a tough-looking stranger with tattoos bending down to pick the wallet up, a kind expression; above his head a glowing GREEN neon "RETURNS IT" with an arrow. Text in the image: only "RETURNS IT".
```

- **If a character drifts off-model,** reply: "Keep YOU exactly like the reference sheet: purple crew-neck T-shirt, short messy black hair, round face, no hoodie."
- **Make 2 versions per episode** ("make a second version with a closer camera"). We'll put both into YouTube's *Test & Compare*.
