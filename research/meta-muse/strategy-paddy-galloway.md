# Paddy Galloway, "2-Hour YouTube Masterclass" (Open Residency) — rules extracted for an automated pipeline

Source: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-SecretXperience/8460bcca-9a56-510a-bf0b-925d0f9090f3/scratchpad/yt2/Z2uoA3bhJT0.transcript.txt` (chapters from the matching `.info.json`). Timestamps are chapter starts; the transcript has no inline timecodes.

## 1. Operating model of the platform
- YouTube is a "click and watch platform"; the algorithm rewards three things: "people clicking, people watching, and people being satisfied." (01:00)
- "The click part of the equation is 50% of the game." Big clients get up to 50 thumbnails per video. (01:00)
- "Virality can be engineered. It's actually a lot more predictable than people think." (01:00)
- The algorithm "is always right behind the audience" — it promotes whatever gets clicked/watched; no need to game format. TV is now 50–60% of client traffic (was 15–20% five years ago), so longer videos win on TV because viewers choose them there. (112:14)
- Traffic sources for a typical channel: search 5–10%, subscriber feed <5%, channel page ~3%. Therefore never optimise titles for SEO scores. (87:42)
- Views are a "comprehensive metric": click, watch and satisfaction all roll into them. (97:28)

## 2. Idea ceiling, floor, TAM
- Three concentric circles: all viewers you could reach (niche TAM) > ceiling set by the idea > execution. The idea caps views "before you film." (09:03)
- Floor = views a "trash video" titled "video one" would get; that number is your core audience size. (09:03, 21:02)
- Any niche can go broad: reframe narrow how-to topics as comparison/scale formats (e.g. "iPhone vs expensive camera", "10 min vs 1 hour vs 100 hours"). (09:03)
- Pick the niche at the intersection of your skills and current demand, then verify demand by finding small channels overperforming. (16:36)
- Subscribers are a "vanity metric"; expect ~20% of subscriber count as normal views per video; above that = overperforming channel. (16:36)
- "Start narrow and gradually expand"; "you can't really start broad and go narrow." Adjacent-topic overlap is overestimated (Call of Duty vs Battlefield audiences overlap ~20%). (29:17)

## 3. The three viewers (CCN framework)
- Core (ride-or-die, ~3–5% of total viewers), Casual (watch 1 in 3–4 uploads), New (everyone else in the niche TAM). Every video must be enjoyable by all three. (21:02)
- Worked example (Jesser): core-only video 400K, core+casual ~1M, core+casual+new 14M. New-viewer reach is what moves outcomes. (21:02)
- Don't take ideas from the comment section: it asks for "a faster horse" (more of the old thing). (21:02)
- "Effort bias": visible high effort ("5 NBA games in 50 hours") attracts new viewers. (21:02)
- Keep a throughline; "if you're just making whatever goes viral in a given moment" you lose core. (21:02)

## 4. Hallmarks of 10M–100M-view videos (filters for idea scoring)
- CCN fit. (33:24)
- Glance test: "you can always just say them in one second." If the idea can't be stated as a one-line log line, "it's not a good idea. It's just a random thought." (33:24)
- Universal appeal: interesting to "the biggest YouTuber in the world and my mother at the same time." (33:24)
- No insider assumptions: never "you probably saw my last video"; sew context in naturally ("Katie, I know you're my girlfriend, but…"). (33:24)
- "They always pose a question that you have to click to answer." Target the feeling of needing to "click to unpause." (33:24, 73:18)

## 5. Three sources of ideas
- Internal: what already worked for you; "do it again," twist it, repeat yearly. Content moves fast, viewers forget. (40:11)
- External ("outlier ideation"): videos at ≥3–4x their channel's average, in your niche and adjacent niches, over the last 365 days; compare the outlier to the same channel's 100K videos to isolate what differed (usually title/thumbnail/format, not editing). Combine adjacent-niche outliers (fishing → "golf with wooden clubs"). (40:11)
- Innovation: combine two existing things into a new format (Hollywood video essays + business whiteboard = YouTube video essays). Cap at ~20% of output. (40:11)
- Sequencing for a new channel: external first → internal once you have data → innovation at scale. Even a 5K video on a 1K-average channel counts as internal data. (40:11)
- From scratch: "find every single outlier in this niche and adjacent niches over the last two years, maybe there's like a thousand," and write your spin on each. (40:11)
- "Frankenstein" packaging: title adapted from one source (Hormozi), thumbnail from another (Canadian cooking channel), concept from a third (Daniel Mac) → 5M views. Keep a swipe file (playlists: outlier, title, thumbnail, intro, retention) and review it 3–5 hours/week. (49:04, 145:22)

## 6. 100 ideas → 10 → 1 (the "1-10-1" / "110 one" funnel)
- Generate 100+ ideas per video (agency: 200–500 per client per month; client sees 5). (52:31)
- 80% strategy rule: topics with ~80% audience overlap ("can 80% of people who watch this video watch this video?"), 80% of the time; experiment 1 in 4–5 videos. (52:31)
- Elimination criteria: 4–5 hard checks per channel, e.g. feasible? can we title/thumbnail it? (kills ~2/3 of ideas), meets aspirational view target? evidence it works (outlier data)? (52:31)
- Calibration: if 100 → 30 survive, criteria too loose; if 100 → 1–2, too strict; aim for ~10. (52:31)
- For each of the 10: a one-page "idea slide" with rough thumbnail sketch, title(s), skeleton, log line. (52:31)
- Pick the one you'd "put your career on." (52:31)
- Guest/topic gate: if it won't rank at least ~5 of 10 against recent uploads, skip unless there's a strategic reason. (80:20)

## 7. Packaging economics
- Nonlinear returns: "a 20% better thumbnail, 40x the views." Gabe's existing video went 1,000 → 40,000 views/day within a day of a thumbnail swap. (62:42)
- Budget 10–20% of total production hours to packaging (10–20 of 100 hours); 50 titles is normal. (62:42)
- Prior-video satisfaction also drives clicks. When evolving a recognisable thumbnail style, change ~20% at a time to keep brand equity. (62:42)
- Packaging is "the same game" as performance marketing: tweak, test, measure. (73:18)

## 8. Packaging workflow (order matters)
1. Idea survives the funnel. (73:18)
2. Titles first, minimum 10; title informs the thumbnail. (73:18)
3. Thumbnail brief before shooting: ~5 concepts, sketched or AI-generated, listing exact shots/angles to capture. (73:18)
4. Thumbnail shoot: 10–30 shots per concept (300–400 photos). (73:18)
5. Design ≥3 differentiated thumbnails (not shirt-colour variants). "Minimum 10 titles, three thumbnails" was a contract line item. (73:18, 80:20)
- Thumbnail craft: "pause and action" (looks like the first frame of the video), leading lines, simple/clean, don't duplicate the title's info, a famous face isn't required if the scene carries it. (73:18)

## 9. Title rules
- Fewest words that stay interesting; under ~60 characters is the soft limit (previously 50); a longer title that adds human interest beats a shorter one. (87:42)
- Universal language: avoid regional/insider terms ("D1 athlete", "Goodwill", "EBITDA" — ~5% of people know it). (87:42)
- Complement, don't repeat, the thumbnail text ("how anyone can go viral" + "this is the key"). (87:42)
- Readable at a glance: short common words ("modern" not "contemporary"), Title Case not ALL CAPS, no three-segment titles. (87:42)
- Title ladder: generic → add story ("I found") → add superlative ("cheapest") → add contradiction ("...but nobody will buy it"). (87:42)
- SEO example: "Don't waste your money: iPhone 16 review" beats keyword-stuffed titles while still ranking. (87:42)

## 10. Publish, predict, panic
- Before publishing, record predicted views at 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week and an expected rank among the last 10 uploads (the "1 of 10" scale). (80:20)
- Publish with the single strongest title plus 3 differentiated thumbnails in native A/B testing. (80:20)
- Panic rule: judge against expectation, not channel average. Expected 1/10 but opening at 4/10 → stop the test, make a dramatic change (new thumbnails, then titles). Expected low but ranking 8/10 → leave it. (80:20)
- Data hierarchy: "good data is always better than no data" (don't touch a winner); "no data is better than bad data" (change a loser now, don't wait). (80:20)
- Don't change the title 10 times in day one; attribution becomes impossible. (80:20)
- Let the A/B test select the winner, lock it, only then test titles if still under expectation. (80:20)

## 11. The only two metrics
- Views and the retention curve. Ignore CTR and AVD dashboards: CTR falls as the audience broadens (lemonade stand in Times Square), AVD is gamed by length. (97:28)
- Retention curves drop as a video reaches new viewers, but drop-off *locations* stay consistent across audiences, so fix the moments, not the level. (97:28)
- Conclusion language ("at the end of the day") triggers exits anywhere in the video; ban it from scripts. (97:28)

## 12. The 45-second intro
- Intro under 45 seconds, simple language, lead with proof. (103:08)
- Step 1 Deliver on the promise in the first 10 seconds, visually if possible ("you made the right choice"); videos can lose 60–70% in 10 seconds when the promise isn't reaffirmed. (103:08)
- Step 2 Create intrigue: open multiple curiosity hooks with the least context possible; "drip feed context" later (MrBeast: "This is an FBI agent. He's got 24 hours to catch me, go."). (103:08)
- Step 3 Seamless flow: "the best intro doesn't feel like an intro"; end with a connecting sentence into the first beat. (103:08)

## 13. Storytelling and stakes
- "Storytelling is useless without stakes." Stake types: enemy/doubter, time limit, reward, punishment, FOMO/cost of not paying attention. (117:26)
- Stakes must fit the video: for informational content use cost-of-ignorance ("the difference ... is over $3 million in retirement"), not gimmicks. (117:26)
- Signal effort; frame contradictions to create human interest. (21:02, 87:42)

## 14. Channel strategy and the pendulum
- Don't run a channel without an 18–24 month commitment; a half-done brand channel is worse than none. (121:36)
- Hire a person who has grown a channel, not a videographer. (121:36)
- Pendulum: (1) make videos merely watchable (audio/picture), (2) shift to idea/title/thumbnail — "make the top 20 with your own twist," (3) only then raise production quality. (130:12)
- Sign a channel only if the goal is "the biggest channel in the niche." Consistency ("again and again") is the skill. (145:22)
- Monetisation phases: ads + sponsors → digital products → physical products; "everything is still downstream of the views." (145:22)

## 15. AI, faceless and AI-made content
- AI used heavily in pre-production/ideation: Claude connected to vidIQ/1of10 via MCP ("find the top 20 most viewed videos in the last 365 days on this topic, then brainstorm"). Gemini for thumbnail image generation and concepts. (136:09)
- "The one area we have not been able to successfully use AI is still writing good content ... we get it like 80% of the way there, but ... the 20% is always still human." Little use in post-production. (136:09)
- Not covered: faceless or fully AI-generated channels are not discussed. The only adjacent point is that recognisable faces aren't required in thumbnails (73:18) and that AI is dissolving editor/graphics gatekeeping for small creators (112:14).

## Encode this
1. Idea gate: reject any idea that can't be stated as a one-sentence log line and a thumbnail concept; require ≥3 of 5 criteria (feasible, packageable, hits CCN, meets view target, outlier evidence).
2. Outlier miner: pull videos ≥3x their channel's average from niche + adjacent niches over 365 days (2 years when cold-starting); diff each outlier against that channel's median video to extract the packaging delta.
3. Funnel ratio: generate 100 ideas per published video, filter to ~10 one-page idea slides, pick 1; auto-flag criteria as too loose (>30 survive) or too strict (<3).
4. Topic mix: 80% of videos must share ~80% audience overlap with the channel's throughline; ≤20% experiments; ≤20% "innovation" ideas.
5. Packaging order: 10 titles → thumbnail brief (5 concepts) → ≥3 differentiated thumbnails; budget 10–20% of pipeline effort here.
6. Title linter: ≤60 chars soft limit, Title Case, no jargon/regional terms, no duplicate of thumbnail text, score for story + superlative + contradiction, never optimise for SEO.
7. Intro template: ≤45 s; promise reaffirmed (visually) in first 10 s; ≥2 open hooks; minimal context; connecting sentence into beat one; ban "at the end of the day"-style closure phrases.
8. Stakes check: every script must declare one stake type (enemy, time, reward, punishment, cost of ignorance) matched to the content type.
9. Pre-publish prediction + panic trigger: log predicted 1h/1d/1w views and expected 1-of-10 rank; if rank is ≥3 places below expectation after the first window, stop the A/B test and swap all thumbnails, then titles; never touch a video performing at or above expectation.
10. Analytics loop on views and retention-curve drop points only; treat CTR/AVD as diagnostics, not targets; feed fixed drop-point patterns back into the script template.
